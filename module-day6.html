The Foundation

Why medical records matter — and what happens without them
📂 Context: How Cases Work

A personal injury case lives or dies on documentation. The adjuster doesn't know what happened to your client — you have to prove it.

Medical records are that proof. They show what injuries the client suffered, what treatment they received, how long they treated, and what it cost. Every demand letter, every negotiation, every settlement is built on this foundation.

Types of Records & How to Request Them

Know what you need before you ask for it

⚖️ Why Complete Billing Matters — O.C.G.A. § 51-12-1.1

Under O.C.G.A. § 51-12-1.1 (added by Georgia’s 2025 Tort Reform Act), recoverable special damages are limited to the reasonable value of medically necessary care — the trier of fact decides that value, and both the amount charged and the amount necessary to satisfy the charge under the client’s insurance are admissible on the question. This applies only to causes of action arising on or after April 21, 2025; earlier accidents fall under prior law.

Practical consequence for records work: an incomplete billing file cannot support a reasonable-value argument. Missing the physician bill or the radiology bill does not just lower the number — it removes the evidence needed to defend it.

Never tell a client “insurance can limit your settlement.” It is inaccurate. Say: “Georgia law limits recoverable medical damages to the reasonable value of necessary treatment; your attorney handles how we prove that value.”

⚠️ RULE-PHYSICIAN-BILL — Three Bills, Not One

When a client had imaging — X-ray, CT, MRI — the hospital’s facility bill is not the whole bill. There are typically three separate charges, from three separate billing entities:

  • Facility bill — the hospital’s charge for the room, equipment, and staff.
  • Physician bill — the treating or attending physician’s professional charge. Billed separately, often by a physician group, not the hospital.
  • Radiology bill — the radiologist’s charge for reading the imaging. Separate from the charge for taking it.

The standard: whenever a client had imaging, request the physician bill AND the radiology bill separately, in addition to the facility bill. This is a records-retrieval completeness requirement, not a judgment call.

Why it matters: the facility bill alone understates the special damages. If you demand on the facility bill only, you have understated your own client’s case — and the missing bills tend to surface after settlement, when nothing can be done about them.

Checklist item: imaging in the records but only one bill in the file = the file is incomplete. Go get the other two.
