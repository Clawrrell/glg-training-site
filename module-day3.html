Why Insurance Knowledge Is Non-Negotiable

Insurance = The Source of Money

💰 In personal injury, the defendant rarely pays out of pocket. Insurance policies are the source of recovery. If you don't understand the policies, you can't maximize the recovery. Every dollar your client receives flows through an insurance policy.

Your job starts with knowing what coverage exists, what the limits are, and who owes what. Intake collects the policies. CMs and LAs track them. Attorneys leverage them. Everyone must understand the fundamentals.

The Building Analogy — How Case Value Works

🏠 Think of it this way: The accident gives you the skeleton of a house. Every piece of evidence is a brick. Treatment, injuries, and liability determine how sturdy the house gets — you build the case brick by brick. The goal is every dollar available IF THE CASE ALLOWS IT. Limits tell you how big the lot is — case value tells you how much house you can build on it.

Limits ≠ Case Value. A $100,000 policy limit doesn't mean the case is worth $100,000. It means the maximum available from that policy is $100,000. The case value depends on injuries, treatment, liability, and documentation. Sometimes the case is worth more than the limits. Sometimes it's worth less. Your job is to build the strongest case possible.

The UM Forced-Investment Analogy

💡 UM is a forced investment in something bad happening. Georgia law makes us pay for it. So when the bad thing happens, we cash in on the investment we were forced to make. When a client says "I don't want to use my own insurance — will my rates go up?" — this is the answer. They've been paying for this coverage. It's theirs to use. Under O.C.G.A. § 33-9-40, Georgia law prohibits insurers from surcharging your premium or canceling your policy when you weren't at fault — and UM claims by definition involve a not-at-fault scenario. Note: the law does not prevent nonrenewal at the end of a policy term, though this is uncommon for a single UM claim.

BI Framing — Every Dollar Available IF the Case Allows It

🏠 The goal is every dollar available IF THE CASE ALLOWS IT. Limits tell you the ceiling. Case value tells you how close to the ceiling you can reach. A $50,000 limit case with $80,000 in documented damages and clear liability? That's a policy-limits demand. A $50,000 limit case with $8,000 in treatment and shared liability? That settles well below limits. Same policy, different cases.

Georgia Minimums, Primary vs. Excess & Multi-Policy Rules

Georgia Minimum Coverage Requirements

CoverageMinimum Per PersonMinimum Per Accident
Bodily Injury (BI)$25,000$50,000
Property Damage (PD)$25,000
UM/UIMEqual to BI limits (unless rejected in writing)
⚠️ Georgia requires UM/UIM equal to BI limits unless the insured specifically rejected it in writing. If there's no written rejection on file, the UM/UIM coverage exists at the full BI limit — even if the declarations page says otherwise. This is a common source of additional recovery.

Primary vs. Excess — Which Policy Pays First

📊 Every coverage type has a priority order. When multiple policies exist (and they often do), you must determine which is PRIMARY (pays first) and which is EXCESS (pays after the primary is exhausted). Getting this wrong means pursuing the wrong insurer and wasting time.

🥇 BI Priority

The at-fault driver's own policy is always primary. The at-fault driver's employer's policy may be excess if the accident was work-related.

🥈 UM/UIM Priority

When multiple UM policies exist (client's auto + spouse's auto + household member's auto), priority is determined by the specific policy language and Georgia stacking rules.

📋 MedPay Priority

MedPay on the vehicle the client was in is primary. MedPay on the client's own policy is secondary if riding in someone else's car.

⚖️ Why It Matters

Pursuing an excess carrier before the primary is exhausted = claim denial. The order of pursuit determines whether the money flows.

CasePeer Multi-Policy Entry — HARD REQUIREMENT

🚨 When multiple UM policies exist — or multiple policies of ANY kind — each policy MUST be entered in CasePeer in the appropriate area so policies are differentiated and primacy can be determined. Do NOT lump multiple policies into one entry. Each policy gets its own line with: insurer name, policy number, coverage type, limits, and the named insured. Without this, the attorney cannot determine which policy to pursue first.

The Claims Process & Working with Adjusters

How the Insurance Claim Process Works

1️⃣ Report & Assign

Accident reported to insurer → claim number assigned → adjuster assigned. GLG sends LOR immediately to establish representation.

2️⃣ Investigate

Adjuster reviews police report, statements, coverage. They're building THEIR version of the case — so are we.

3️⃣ Evaluate & Reserve

Adjuster sets an internal "reserve" — their estimated payout. This number is never disclosed to us. Our demand challenges it.

4️⃣ Negotiate & Resolve

Demand sent → counter-offers exchanged → settlement or litigation. The CM-track modules cover this process in depth.

The Coverage Discovery Process

🔍 At intake, collect ALL insurance information: at-fault driver's BI/PD policy, client's own UM/UIM, client's MedPay, any commercial/umbrella policies, household member policies that may provide additional UM coverage. Request declarations pages from every insurer. Every policy missed at intake is money potentially left on the table.

The GLG Insurance Verification Process

Coverage Verification — The GLG Standard

📞 Step 1: Contact Insurers

Call every insurer identified at intake. Verify policy status, coverage types, limits, and named insureds. Request declarations pages.

📋 Step 2: Enter in CasePeer

Each policy entered separately in the appropriate CasePeer area. BI under defendant's insurance. UM/UIM under client's insurance. Each with limits, policy number, adjuster name/contact.

🔍 Step 3: Identify Gaps

Is there UM/UIM? Was it rejected in writing? Are there additional household policies? Commercial policies? Umbrella? Every gap is a question for the attorney.

✅ Step 4: Flag for Attorney

Present the complete coverage picture. Attorney determines: which policies to pursue, in what order (primary → excess), and whether additional investigation is needed.

Common Mistakes That Cost Clients Money

⚠️ Top 5 insurance mistakes at PI firms:
1. Not checking for UM/UIM coverage on the client's own policy
2. Assuming the at-fault driver has minimum limits without verifying
3. Missing additional household policies that provide UM stacking
4. Not requesting declarations pages (relying on verbal confirmations)
5. Entering multiple policies as one CasePeer entry (losing primacy tracking)

The Client Conversation About Insurance

💬 Clients often resist using their own UM/UIM: "Why am I using MY insurance? The other driver hit ME!" Your response: UM is a forced investment in something bad happening — Georgia law makes us pay for it, so when the bad thing happens, we cash in on the investment we were forced to make. Under O.C.G.A. § 33-9-40, your insurer cannot surcharge your premium or cancel your policy for a not-at-fault claim. Nonrenewal at term end is technically possible but uncommon for a single UM claim.

The BI Conversation — When Clients Hesitate to Pursue the At-Fault Driver

💬 Some clients feel guilty pursuing the at-fault driver's insurance: "I don't want to take money from them — what if their rates go up?" Your response: You're not taking money from anyone personally — you're using coverage the at-fault driver paid premiums for, for years, for exactly this situation. If you don't pursue it, that money doesn't go back to them — it stays with the insurance company that collected those premiums for this exact purpose. That's what liability insurance is for. The at-fault driver's rate consequences are between them and their insurer — not your responsibility, and not a reason to leave your medical bills unpaid.
