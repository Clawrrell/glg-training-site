Demand Writing: Attorney Judgment

The demand letter is not a summary of damages. It is the single most important persuasion document in the case.
PA Track — Module 2

Two identical cases. Two very different outcomes.

Case A: $48,000 in specials, clear liability, $100K limits. The demand went out at $52,000 with a two-page letter listing medical bills. The adjuster countered at $28,000. After three rounds, the case settled for $36,000. The client netted $19,000 after fees and costs.

Case B: Same specials, same liability, same limits. The demand went out at $95,000 with a narrative that identified the specific safety rule the defendant violated, explained how the force of the collision traveled through the vehicle into the client\u2019s spine, established the client as a working mother and community volunteer, and presented comparable jury verdicts before stating the demand figure. The adjuster countered at $62,000. The case settled for $78,000. The client netted $47,000.

Same facts. Same injuries. Same coverage. $28,000 difference in client recovery. The only variable was the demand strategy.

Section 2 · Demand Strategy & Valuation · Up to 40 pts

How to Value a Case and Set the Demand Number

The demand number is not a wish. It is a strategic anchor that frames the entire negotiation. Too low and you never recover. Too high without documentation and you lose credibility.
The Anchor Problem

You can never negotiate UP from a low demand.

When a demand goes out at $35,000 on a case worth $80,000, the negotiation is already lost. The adjuster\u2019s counter will be anchored to your number, not to the case value. Even aggressive negotiation from a low starting point cannot recover the ground lost by the initial anchor. The demand number is the single most consequential decision in pre-litigation.

🔍 Coverage Maximization — The Evaluation Layer Most Attorneys Miss

  • Demand affidavit: When a defendant has $250K+ in liability limits, demand a sworn affidavit confirming no additional coverage. Defendants with $250K+ limits frequently carry a $1M umbrella policy.
  • UM/UIM stacking: Check for add-on vs. traditional UM coverage. Add-on stacks; traditional offsets. Some carriers stack every vehicle as a separate policy.
  • Resident relative policies: Every household member\u2019s auto policy may provide additional UM/UIM coverage.
  • Commercial policies: If the defendant was working, the employer\u2019s commercial policy applies — frequently the difference between a $25K case and a $1M case.
  • MedPay: First-party medical payment coverage. Collect early to fund treatment and preserve health insurance subrogation leverage.
When to Demand Aggressively vs. Conservatively: Demand at or near policy limits when the Safety Checklist score is 8–10, liability is clear, injuries are significant, and specials approach or exceed limits. Demand conservatively when comparative negligence exposure exists, treatment gaps weaken causation, or coverage is limited. A conservative demand is not a weak demand — it is a realistic anchor that preserves credibility and accelerates resolution on cases where aggressive posture would backfire.
Fee-to-Cost Ratio: Before investing resources, evaluate whether the expected recovery justifies the firm\u2019s costs. The ideal ratio is 10:1 (legal fees to case disbursements). A case where the expected fee is $4,000 and projected costs are $2,000 (ratio 2:1) requires careful consideration — aggressive demand preparation may eat into already-thin margins. Quick resolution at a fair number may serve the client better than an extended negotiation.

Section 3 · Safety Rule Framing · Up to 40 pts

Making the Adjuster Care About the Community, Not Just the Plaintiff

A demand that describes an accident is worth X. A demand that describes a safety rule violation that endangered the community is worth 3X. Same facts. Different frame.
The Framing Gap

Two demand openings. Same case. Completely different adjuster reactions.

Demand A: "On March 15, 2025, the defendant rear-ended our client at a red light, causing injuries."

Demand B: "Every driver on Georgia roads is required to maintain awareness of the road ahead and stop when traffic requires it. This rule protects every driver, passenger, and pedestrian in the community. On March 15, 2025, the defendant violated this rule. Our client paid the price. The community has an interest in ensuring that safety violations carry real consequences."

Demand A describes an event. Demand B identifies a rule violation that endangered everyone. The adjuster who reads Demand B evaluates the case as a community enforcement issue, not just an individual claim.

⚖️ The Five Elements of Safety Rule Framing

1Identify the specific safety rule. "A driver must maintain a safe following distance." "A property owner must fix known hazards." If you cannot state the rule in one sentence, the adjuster (and later, the jury) will not remember it.
2Separate mechanism of wreck from mechanism of harm. The wreck is what happened to the vehicles. The harm is what happened inside the human body. In low-speed impacts, vehicle damage may be minimal while force transfer to the occupant is significant. A ton of metal at 6 mph delivers greater force than dropping that weight on a person from more than a foot high.
3Credential the client. Before presenting damages, establish the plaintiff as a community member worth protecting. "Works, pays taxes, feeds their kids." Once the adjuster sees the plaintiff as worthy, the damages feel proportionate.
4Apply the random victim test. "Who else could have been hurt?" If the defendant\u2019s conduct endangered anyone in the community, the case is not about one plaintiff — it is about community safety enforcement.
5Polarize the defense position. Force the defense to commit: "Either the client is lying about their injuries, or the defendant\u2019s conduct caused real harm to a real person." Make the defense position look untenable. Do not let them occupy comfortable middle ground.
Pre-Existing Conditions Are Multipliers, Not Defenses. Under the eggshell plaintiff doctrine, every driver must drive as if every other vehicle contains the most fragile possible person. A pre-existing condition does not reduce the defendant\u2019s liability — it increases it. The demand should frame pre-existing conditions aggressively: "The defendant\u2019s conduct was especially dangerous because it aggravated a condition that was previously manageable and asymptomatic."
Safety Rule Demand Evaluation Checklist (Pre-Lit Head Attorney SOP §1): Every demand you review must pass these five questions before submission: (1) Has the safety rule violation been clearly identified? (2) Does the demand explain the mechanism of harm? (3) Does the demand establish community interest? (4) Has the random victim framing been applied? (5) Is the demand value anchored high enough? If any demand fails these questions, return it for revision.

Section 4 · Narrative Sequencing · Up to 40 pts

Backward Mapping, Information Order & Proactive Weakness Management

A demand figure presented without context is evaluated on its own. A figure presented after a fully-built narrative is evaluated relative to everything the adjuster has already absorbed.
The Contrast Phenomenon

A spa company changed one thing about its sales pitch and sales increased 520%.

They reversed the presentation order. Instead of showing the service price first and then describing the benefits, they described the benefits first — the experience, the results, the transformation — and THEN revealed the price. The price did not change. The context the buyer had absorbed before seeing the price changed everything.

The same principle governs demand writing. Present comparable jury verdicts in the same county — showing the adjuster what a jury awarded in a similar case — BEFORE stating your demand figure. The adjuster evaluates your number relative to the verdicts they just read, not in isolation.

📋 Recommended Demand Narrative Sequence

1Open with the human story. Client background — what their life looked like before the accident. Prime the reader\u2019s attention toward the person, not the claim number.
2Establish liability clearly. Eliminate uncertainty about who is at fault before presenting damages. If the adjuster is still questioning liability when they reach the damages section, they will discount everything.
3Build the severity contrast. Mechanism of injury, emergency treatment, pain severity. Build the emotional context that makes the damages figure feel proportionate, not inflated.
4Present medical damages with authority. Treating physicians, diagnostic findings, bills organized chronologically. Include the specials summary with exact totals.
5Then present the demand figure. After the reader has absorbed liability, injury severity, and medical treatment. At this point, the number has a frame — it is evaluated relative to everything above, not as a standalone ask.

🎯 Backward Mapping from the Decisive Event

Before drafting, identify the decisive event — the single moment most likely to produce a favorable resolution. In most pre-litigation cases, that moment is the adjuster receiving the demand and authorizing full settlement authority.

Backward map from that moment: What does the adjuster need to see to authorize payment? Documented liability, complete medical records, credible specials calculation, compelling narrative, and a demand anchored high enough to allow movement. Build every element of the demand package to make that moment successful.

Proactive Weakness Management: Every case has weaknesses — treatment gaps, pre-existing conditions, comparative fault arguments. Address them in the demand BEFORE the adjuster raises them. Explain the treatment gap as physician-directed conservative care. Frame pre-existing conditions as multipliers under the eggshell plaintiff doctrine. Anticipate and neutralize the defense arguments so the adjuster reads your explanation first, not theirs.

Section 5 · Holt Demand Compliance · Up to 40 pts

O.C.G.A. § 9-11-67.1 — The Statutory Weapon

A valid Holt demand that is rejected — and later exceeded at judgment — triggers mandatory attorney\u2019s fees and costs against the defendant. Missing ONE component forfeits the entire weapon.
The $47,000 Mistake

A demand with five of six components is not a Holt demand. It is a standard settlement offer with no teeth.

The firm sent a $200,000 demand to a trucking company\u2019s insurer. Strong liability. Documented injuries. The demand letter contained five of the six required Holt components — but stated "policy limits" instead of the specific dollar amount ($200,000.00). The insurer rejected. The case went to trial and the jury returned a $320,000 verdict. The firm sought $47,000 in attorney\u2019s fees and costs under § 9-11-67.1. The court denied the motion. The demand was not a valid Holt demand because it lacked Component 3 — a specific dollar amount. "Policy limits" without a number does not satisfy the statute.

⚖️ The Six Required Components of a Holt Demand

#ComponentRequirementCommon Mistake
1Written OfferMust be in writing — verbal demands do not qualifyVerbal demand without written follow-up
2Time Period for AcceptanceMust specify acceptance period — minimum 30 daysNo stated deadline, or deadline under 30 days
3Amount of Monetary DamagesMust state a specific dollar amount — not a rangeDemanding "policy limits" without the actual dollar figure
4Parties to Be ReleasedMust identify each party to be releasedVague "all parties" language without names
5Claims to Be ReleasedMust specify which claims are being releasedGeneric "all claims" without reference to the incident
6Persons to Be BoundMust identify who will be bound by the settlementFailing to bind spouse (loss of consortium) or heirs

Pre-Send Verification (Holt Demand Checklist): Before any Holt demand leaves the office: (1) All six components present and expressly stated. (2) Specific dollar amount — not "policy limits" without a number. (3) All defendants and parties named. (4) All claimants bound including spouse/heirs. (5) 30-day acceptance period stated. (6) Sent via certified mail AND email with delivery confirmation. (7) Copy uploaded to CasePeer. (8) 30-day follow-up task set. Two sets of eyes — attorney reviews, CM verifies.

Section 6 · Post-Demand Negotiation · Up to 40 pts

The Seven-Stage Model, Anchoring & Counter-Offer Strategy

Most failed negotiations fail at Stage 1 (insufficient preparation). The phone call determines how close to the ceiling the case lands.
The Preparation Principle

The negotiation is decided before the phone rings.

No first-offer call happens without the pre-negotiation worksheet completed. The worksheet captures: the adjuster\u2019s likely reserve, their authority level, case BATNA (both sides), the client\u2019s walk-away number, the true floor, the pseudo-floor, concession increments, and the walk-away trigger. A case manager negotiating without the worksheet is improvising with the client\u2019s money. The attorney sets the worksheet; the CM executes it.

1 — PreparationKnow specials, limits, target range, true floor, pseudo-floor, concession increments, walk-away triggerPre-set discipline — the negotiation is decided before the phone rings

🧠 Psychological Principles in Every Negotiation

  • Anchoring: The demand number frames the entire negotiation. A demand that anchors too low permanently limits recovery. High anchor plus documented justification equals better outcomes.
  • Loss Aversion: Adjusters feel the pain of paying out more sharply than the relief of closing a file. Frame settlement as avoiding the risk of a much larger verdict — a loss prevented, not just money spent.
  • Reactive Devaluation: Adjusters reflexively discount plaintiff demands. Counter by framing settlement as objective (documented medical damages plus multiplier plus comparable verdicts), not as an arbitrary ask.
  • Tactical Empathy: Understand what drives the adjuster\u2019s evaluation without agreeing with it. Label their concerns: "It sounds like you feel this case has some liability issues." Disarms defensiveness and opens dialogue.
  • Calibrated Questions: "How did you arrive at that number?" forces the adjuster to defend their position and often reveals their real ceiling. Never counter immediately — first understand their reasoning.
The Counter-Offer Rule: Never come down more than 50% of the gap between your last number and the adjuster\u2019s offer. Example: You demanded $75K. Adjuster offers $25K. Gap = $50K. Your counter must be at least $50K ($75K - $25K/2). This ensures you are not giving ground faster than they are. Move in decreasing increments and end on an odd, specific number ($47,350 rather than $47,500) — specific numbers signal you have done your math.
Escalation Triggers: Escalate to the attorney when: the adjuster\u2019s best offer does not clear specials, liability is being entirely denied, 3+ rounds with no meaningful movement, the adjuster is acting in bad faith, or the SOL is within 6 months.
