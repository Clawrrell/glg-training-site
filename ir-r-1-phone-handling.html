Section 2 · The GLG Greeting · Up to 40 pts

Standard Greeting, Tone & the 3-Ring Rule

Every call starts the same way. No exceptions. No shortcuts.

📞 The Standard Greeting (Verbatim)

"Thank you for calling Gunn Law Group, this is [Your Name], how can I help you?"

This greeting is not a suggestion. It is the standard. Every call. Every time.

🎯 Why This Greeting Works

  • "Thank you for calling" — opens with warmth and gratitude
  • "Gunn Law Group" — anchors the caller's choice and establishes firm authority
  • "This is [Your Name]" — personal connection builds immediate trust
  • "How can I help you?" — invites engagement, puts the caller in control
The 3-Ring Rule: Answer all incoming calls within 3 rings. The target is 95%+ compliance. Every ring past three signals to the caller: "This firm might not have their act together."

🗣️ Tone Matters More Than Perfect Words

  • Smile when you answer — people can hear it in your voice
  • Speak at a measured pace — not rushed, not dragging
  • Match the caller's energy appropriately — don't rush a scared caller, don't slow-walk an urgent one
  • Never answer while eating, having side conversations, or distracted

Section 4 · Messages & Follow-Up · Up to 40 pts

Message Taking, Voicemail & the 15-Minute Relay Rule

A message addressed to "the team" is assigned to no one. Every message goes to a named person, within 15 minutes.
Real-World Consequence

A client calls at 10:15 AM about an urgent question on their upcoming deposition. The receptionist takes a message and puts it in the "general inbox." By 3:00 PM, no one has seen it. The client calls back — angry, anxious, and questioning whether the firm cares about their case.

The message sat for nearly 5 hours because it was sent to "the team" instead of the assigned paralegal by name. A task addressed to a team is assigned to no one. The client's trust dropped. Their cooperation in prep went sideways. All because a message wasn't relayed to a named person within 15 minutes.

📝 Complete Message Checklist

  • Caller's full name
  • Phone number (repeat it back to confirm)
  • Best callback time
  • Brief reason for the call
  • Is it urgent?

Relay to the named recipient within 15 minutes. Use whatever gets seen fastest — CasePeer task, email, Teams, or text.

Voicemail Protocol: Check the firm's main voicemail at the start of every business day AND after lunch. Route all voicemail messages to the appropriate staff member within 30 minutes of checking.
Never promise a specific callback time unless the recipient has confirmed availability. If you promise a callback time, it MUST be honored. Broken promises destroy client trust faster than slow service.

Section 5 · Visitors & Role Boundaries · Up to 40 pts

Greeting Visitors & What Reception Does NOT Do

You greet every person within 30 seconds. You never cross the line into legal territory.

👋 The GLG Welcome System

  • Greet every visitor immediately upon entry — within 30 seconds
  • Standard greeting: "Good morning/afternoon, welcome to Gunn Law Group. How can I help you?"
  • Ask for the visitor's name and purpose of their visit
  • Notify the appropriate staff member immediately with the visitor's name
  • Offer a seat and provide an estimated wait time if applicable
  • Never leave a visitor unattended for more than a few minutes without an update

✨ First Impression Excellence

  • Genuine smile — increases perceived competence and trustworthiness
  • Eye contact — signals respect and attention
  • Stand or lean forward — shows engagement
  • Focus completely — no phone, no side conversations during the greeting
  • Follow through — if you say "5 minutes," check back in 4
🚫 What Reception Does NOT Do:
  • Does NOT provide legal advice, case updates, or settlement information of any kind
  • Does NOT discuss case strategy, liability, or case value
  • Does NOT access or relay information from CasePeer to callers
  • Does NOT make commitments on behalf of attorneys or paralegals
  • Does NOT attempt to resolve client complaints — escalate to supervisor
  • Does NOT route calls directly to Managing Attorney without trying the appropriate department first

When in doubt, route — do not answer.

Section 6 · Handling Difficult Callers · Up to 40 pts

De-Escalation, Routing & the Khalil Incident Rule

The upset caller is a client in crisis. De-escalate, route, document. Never try to be the hero.
The Khalil Incident

A client called asking for a case update. The receptionist — trying to be helpful — told them: "I think your case settled for around $30,000." The actual settlement was $45,000. The client became furious, called back screaming at the attorney, and nearly fired the firm.

The receptionist gave wrong case information with good intentions. The client formed expectations based on incorrect data. When the real number came, instead of being happy about $45K, they were enraged about the "lie." This is why reception NEVER provides case-specific information. Not because you're unhelpful — because even a well-meaning mistake can destroy client trust and damage the case.

🧊 The De-Escalation Protocol (4 Steps)

  1. Step 1 — Emotional Regulation: Stay calm. Lower your voice slightly. Breathe before speaking. Do NOT match their emotional tone.
  2. Step 2 — Acknowledgment: "I understand you're frustrated. Let me get the right person on the line for you." Show empathy without admitting fault.
  3. Step 3 — Professional Boundaries: Do not argue, explain case details, or attempt to resolve the complaint. Route to paralegal or supervisor immediately.
  4. Step 4 — Documentation: Document the call in the message log with a note about the caller being upset and the escalation path taken.
If a caller becomes verbally abusive or threatening: "I want to help, but I'm going to need to transfer you to a supervisor." Transfer immediately to the Managing Attorney. Do not engage further.
