What Changed — The Insurance Affordability Claims Integrity Act

The four pillars of HB 1344 and why every one of them touches PI practice.

⚖️ The Four Pillars of HB 1344

#PillarWhat Changed
1Enhanced Fraud Detection & ReportingMandatory fraud reporting for insurers, agents, and employees. Commissioner can employ prosecuting attorneys for fraud cases. 90-day prosecution timeline.
2Claims Processing & TimingCatastrophic event processing requirements. 60-day homeowner correction period for aerial/satellite imagery issues. Enhanced transparency for premium tax reporting.
3Motor Vehicle InsuranceHigher penalties for driving without minimum coverage. Enhanced UM protections and clarifications. Strengthened enforcement of minimum coverage requirements.
4Regulatory EnforcementExpanded Commissioner authority. Special Insurance Fraud Fund. Inter-agency cooperation requirements. Enhanced PII protections for judges.

🏛 Sovereign Immunity — A Critical Change

HB 1344 addresses state liability: "The state waives its sovereign immunity for the torts of state officers and employees while acting within the scope of their official duties or employment and shall be liable for such torts in the same manner as a private individual or entity would be liable under like circumstances."

This matters for GLG cases involving government vehicles, state employees, or government-related accidents. The state is now liable like a private entity — but only for conduct within the scope of official duties.

🔍 Information Prohibition — New Compliance Requirement

HB 1344 makes it illegal to solicit, release, or sell automobile accident information outside authorized channels. This directly affects:

  • Client intake: CMs must ask whether anyone has contacted the client about the accident
  • Marketing: GLG must ensure marketing practices comply with the new prohibition
  • Case management: Any suspicious third-party contact must be documented and reported
Strategic relevance: HB 1344 changes insurance fraud detection, claims processing timelines, adjuster interaction protocols, and the regulatory environment within which insurance companies operate. These changes affect case evaluation, negotiation strategies, and client outcome expectations across every active case.

Mandatory Reporting, Prosecution Timelines & the Fraud Fund

Who must report, when prosecution must begin, and what is now illegal.

📋 Mandatory Fraud Reporting — The New Standard

Under HB 1344, the reporting obligation is broad and clear:

"Any insurer, agent, or other person licensed under this title, or an employee thereof, having knowledge of or who believes that a fraudulent insurance act is being or has been committed shall send to the Commissioner a report or information pertinent to such knowledge or belief."

Key phrase: "knowledge of or believes" — this is not a certainty standard. A reasonable belief triggers the obligation.

⏱ The 90-Day Prosecution Timeline

StageRequirement
Report FiledInsurer/agent sends fraud report to the Commissioner
Commissioner ReviewCommissioner investigates; may employ prosecuting attorneys specifically for insurance fraud
90-Day WindowProsecuting attorney must begin prosecution within 90 days OR inform the Commissioner of reasons for the delay
AccountabilityIf no prosecution begins, the Commissioner receives an explanation — creating a paper trail of inaction

🚫 Automobile Accident Information Prohibition

HB 1344 creates strict controls on the solicitation, release, or sale of automobile accident information. This means:

  • Third parties cannot cold-call accident victims to solicit case details
  • Accident information cannot be sold or released outside authorized channels
  • Runners and unlicensed case solicitors face potential criminal liability
  • GLG staff must document and report any suspicious contacts clients describe
Why this matters for GLG: When an insurer flags a case for "fraud review," understanding the 90-day timeline gives GLG leverage. The insurer cannot use "fraud investigation" as an indefinite delay tactic — there is now a statutory accountability mechanism.
Special Insurance Fraud Fund: HB 1344 establishes dedicated funding for fraud investigation and prosecution. This means more investigators, more scrutiny, and more consequences — for actual fraud. GLG's documentation standards must be strong enough to withstand this heightened environment.

How HB 1344 Changes Case Evaluation, Negotiation & Coverage

The regulatory shift that every case strategy must account for.

📊 Case Evaluation Under HB 1344

HB 1344 changes the environment in which every PI case operates. During PITT review and case evaluation, staff must now consider:

  • Enhanced fraud detection: Insurers are more likely to flag cases for fraud review — legitimate or not
  • Adjuster behavior changes: Adjusters operate under new reporting obligations and timing pressures
  • Claims processing timelines: New timing requirements prevent indefinite delay tactics
  • Documentation scrutiny: Case files must be stronger because the regulatory environment is more aggressive

💼 Settlement Negotiation Strategy — What Changed

🛡 UM Coverage — Enhanced Protections

HB 1344 enhances uninsured motorist (UM) coverage protections and clarifications. For GLG:

  • Verify UM coverage more carefully — the regulatory framework for coverage has changed
  • Increased penalties for uninsured drivers strengthen the UM claim narrative
  • Enhanced enforcement means fewer gaps in the coverage landscape over time
  • Cross-reference with the Injury Case Playbook's Coverage Maximization Audit — every case needs complete coverage investigation
Critical for all roles: HB 1344 does NOT reduce the viability of PI cases. It changes the environment. Insurers have more oversight, but claimants also have more protections. GLG's advantage is in documentation quality and regulatory awareness — know the rules better than the adjuster.

Updating Your Daily Work for HB 1344 Compliance

Documentation, CasePeer protocols, client communication, and compliance training.

📋 Documentation Standards — Strengthened

Under HB 1344's heightened regulatory environment, GLG's documentation must:

  • Include complete records of all communications with adjusters, providers, and clients
  • Document treatment verification — medical records, provider notes, treatment timelines
  • Preserve all evidence — photos, police reports, witness statements — with even more rigor
  • Flag any suspicious third-party contacts clients report (HB 1344 accident info prohibition)
  • Note any insurer timing issues — delays, processing failures, fraud review invocations

💻 CasePeer Protocol Updates

UpdatePurpose
Fraud Awareness FlagTrack whether the insurer has flagged the case for fraud review. Document date, stated reason, and follow-up.
Solicitation Contact LogLog any suspicious third-party contacts clients report — who called, when, what was asked, contact info if available.
Insurer Timing MonitorTrack claims processing milestones against HB 1344 timing requirements. Document delays with dates and explanations.
Enhanced Scrutiny AlertFlag cases where insurer behavior suggests heightened HB 1344-related scrutiny — triggers additional documentation review.

📞 Client Communication — What Changed

CMs must now incorporate HB 1344 awareness into client conversations:

  • At intake: Ask whether anyone has contacted the client about the accident (solicitation check)
  • During treatment: Explain that insurers have enhanced fraud detection — thorough treatment compliance matters more than ever
  • At settlement: Reassure clients that HB 1344 protects legitimate claimants through enhanced timing requirements
  • If fraud flagged: Explain without alarming — GLG handles compliance; the client focuses on treatment
The advantage: GLG staff who understand HB 1344 can use the new regulatory framework strategically — pushing back on improper delays, strengthening documentation, and reassuring clients. Knowledge of the law is a competitive advantage when the opposing adjuster may not fully understand it either.
