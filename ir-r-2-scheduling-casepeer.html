Section 2 · CasePeer Navigation & Daily Routine · Up to 40 pts

Dashboard, Calendar & the Opening/Closing Checklist

CasePeer is the firm's single source of truth. Your day starts and ends here.

🖥️ The CasePeer Dashboard

When you log in, the dashboard shows you:

  • Assigned tasks — due today, overdue, upcoming
  • Recent activity on cases
  • Calendar events — today's appointments and meetings
  • Quick search bar at the top — find cases, contacts, calendar entries

At reception level, the calendar is your command center. It tells you who's coming in, when attorneys are available, and where scheduling gaps exist.

Physical (Reception SOP §6)CasePeer (Basics Training §8)

Phones active and forwarding testedReview today's calendar & appointments
Voicemail checked and messages routedNote any overdue tasks (for assigned staff)

Both checklists run in parallel. Physical setup AND CasePeer setup — before 9:00 AM, every day.

Closing Routine: Before you leave: all messages relayed AND logged in CasePeer, no orphan tasks (every message has a task to a named person), phones forwarded, desk cleared, unresolved items flagged to supervisor. When you close your laptop, every communication is logged and every task is set.

Section 3 · Communication Notes · Up to 40 pts

Logging Messages in CasePeer & the 15-Minute Relay

"Called client" is not a note. It's a waste of pixels.
Good Note vs Bad Note

Two receptionists. Same message. One logs: "Client called about records." The other logs: "Client Robert Williams called at 2:15 PM at (678) 555-3421. Asking about status of medical records from Dr. Patel — says records were supposed to be ready last week. Has follow-up appointment Thursday. Task set for case manager Tanya — callback today."

When Tanya picks up the first note, she has to call the client just to find out what they wanted. When she picks up the second note, she already knows the issue, the urgency, and can prepare the answer before dialing. The difference between a good note and a bad note is 5 extra minutes of effort — and it saves everyone 30 minutes of confusion.

📝 Every CasePeer Note Must Include

  1. WHO you contacted — name, title, phone number
  2. WHAT was discussed — specific topics, not vague summaries
  3. WHAT WAS DECIDED or learned
  4. NEXT STEPS — and corresponding tasks set in CasePeer
The 15-Minute Relay, Systematized: In IR-R-1 you learned to relay messages within 15 minutes. In CasePeer, this means: log the communication note AND create a task to the named recipient within 15 minutes. CasePeer is the audit trail — the proof that the relay happened.

Section 4 · Task Creation · Up to 40 pts

Creating Tasks, Routing to Named People & Catching Gaps

A task addressed to "the team" is assigned to no one. Every task goes to a named person.
Real-World Consequence

A CasePeer task reads: "Follow up." That's it. No client name. No phone number. No context. No due date. It sits in a paralegal's queue for three days because they don't know what it refers to — and when they finally click into it, they can't figure out what "follow up" even means.

Meanwhile, the client who called three days ago is now furious. They call the Managing Attorney directly. The task existed — but it was useless. A vague task is worse than no task at all because it creates the illusion that something is being handled.

✅ Good Task vs ❌ Bad Task

❌ Bad Task✅ Good Task
"Follow up""Follow up with Dr. Martinez office for final medical records — called 3/15, said 7-10 days"
"Client called""Client Angela Davis called at 10:45 AM, (404) 555-8892 — PD question about rental car return. Needs callback before 3 PM today."
"Check on case""Follow up with State Farm adjuster Jane Doe on demand response — demand sent 3/1"
The Named Person Rule: Every CasePeer task must be assigned to a specific, named person. Not "Pre-Lit Team." Not "Front Desk." Not "Whoever's Available." If you don't know who handles the case, ask — don't assign to a group and hope.

Section 5 · Calendar & Scheduling · Up to 40 pts

Appointments, Availability & Avoiding Double-Bookings

The calendar is your command center. If the appointment isn't in CasePeer, it doesn't exist.
Real-World Consequence

Two clients show up at the same time for the same paralegal. One has a 2:00 PM appointment in CasePeer. The other has a "confirmed" appointment — but it was only written on a desk calendar and never entered in the system.

The paralegal is blindsided. One client waits 45 minutes. The other leaves angry. A desk calendar and CasePeer showed two different realities — and neither client got the experience they deserved. One system. One calendar. CasePeer.

📅 The Scheduling Workflow (3 Steps)

  1. Enter — Add the appointment in CasePeer calendar with client name, time, assigned staff, and reason for visit
  2. Confirm — Read back the details to the client: "You're confirmed for Thursday at 10:00 AM with Attorney Gunn"
  3. Notify — Alert the assigned staff member that a new appointment has been added

All three steps. Every time. Skip one and the system has a gap.

Avoiding Double-Bookings: Always check the CasePeer calendar BEFORE confirming any appointment. If you see a potential overlap, offer alternative times — don't book over existing commitments and hope it works out.

Case Status Awareness & Template Recognition

You see statuses. You use them to route smarter. You never share them with clients.

📊 Case Statuses at a Glance (Read-Only for Reception)

StatusWhat It MeansHow It Helps You at Reception
New / IntakeCase just signedRoute to intake team or assigned paralegal
Active TreatmentClient is actively treatingClient may call about appointments, treatment questions
Records RetrievalGathering medical recordsClient may call about records status — route to case manager
Demand SentDemand delivered to insurerClient is likely anxious about timeline — route to pre-lit paralegal
NegotiationOffers & counters in progressClient may ask about settlement — NEVER disclose, route to paralegal
LitigationLawsuit filedRoute to litigation paralegal, not pre-lit
The Line You Never Cross: Case status is internal routing intelligence. You see it. You use it to write smarter task notes. You NEVER share it with clients. Not even "I think it's in pre-lit." The Reception SOP is absolute: reception does NOT provide case updates of any kind.

📄 Template Awareness (Not Template Creation)

CasePeer uses numbered document templates (Welcome Letters, LOPs, Demand Letters, etc.). At reception level, you should know:

  • Templates exist — they automate standard firm documents
  • What they are — so you can route intelligently when clients ask about them
  • You don't create them — that's the paralegal's or case manager's job
  • You don't discuss them — template activity is case-specific information
