IR-I Track — Module 1

A man calls GLG. His wife was in a car accident six weeks ago. She's in pain, can't work, and the bills are piling up. He's scared. He calls your number because a friend told him you're the best.

You take the call. The man describes the accident — it sounds like a solid case. But he mentions one detail: "She was hit by a city bus." You take a full intake, accept the case, and hand it off to the paralegal.

Because the screener didn't catch the red flag.

Section 2 · Case Type Identification · Up to 40 pts

Accepted vs. Referred: Know What GLG Handles

The first screening question is always: does this case type fall within GLG's practice?

✅ Accepted Case Types (Handle In-House)

  • Motor vehicle accidents (MVA) — car, truck, motorcycle, rideshare, pedestrian, bicycle
  • Premises liability — slip and fall, trip and fall, negligent security, dog bites
  • Wrongful death (arising from MVA or premises liability)
  • Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) claims

🚫 Referred Out — Do Not Accept

  • Workers' compensation
  • Products liability
  • Medical malpractice
  • Non-personal injury matters (family law, criminal, immigration, etc.)

For referred case types: politely explain the firm specializes in personal injury, offer to provide a referral if available, and log the call in CasePeer as a declined lead with the reason noted.

Accepting the wrong case type wastes resources, delays the caller's access to the right attorney, and can create ethical problems. Know the list. Apply it every time.

Section 3 · Screening Criteria & Red Flags · Up to 40 pts

The Four Thresholds, Red Flags & Auto-Declines

Every case must clear four minimum thresholds. Some signals require immediate attorney escalation. Others are automatic declines.
Real-World Consequence

A caller describes an accident with an 18-wheeler. The intake screener takes a full intake, accepts the case, and routes it to the paralegal team. Nobody escalates to the attorney.

Three days later, the trucking company's insurer contacts the driver. By the time the attorney learns it's a trucking case, the electronic logging device data may have already been overwritten. The dashcam footage? Gone. Evidence that could have proven the driver was fatigued — destroyed because no one flagged "commercial vehicle" as a red flag requiring immediate escalation.

📋 Four Minimum Screening Thresholds — ALL Must Be Met

  1. Within the statute of limitations — 2 years for PI in Georgia (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33)
  2. Clear third-party liability — another person or entity was at fault
  3. Medical treatment — client sustained injuries requiring medical treatment
  4. Accepted case type — falls within GLG's practice (Section 2 list)

Plus: real property damage + real injury.

🚩 Red Flags — Escalate to Attorney

  • Client has prior representation on the same matter
  • Government entity involved — ante litem notice deadlines (SAME DAY escalation)
  • Commercial vehicle / trucking — spoliation risk (escalate IMMEDIATELY)
  • Incident involves a minor
  • Client mentions pre-existing conditions at the same body part
  • Conflicting accounts of how the incident occurred

🛑 Automatic Decline

  • Statute of limitations expired
  • No third-party liability (single-car accident with no other party)
  • Client seeking representation for a non-PI matter
  • Client already represented on the same case

Section 4 · SB 68 Seatbelt Check & SOL Awareness · Up to 40 pts

The Seatbelt Question & Statute of Limitations

SB 68 changed the game for MVA lawsuits filed on or after April 21, 2025. And a SOL you miss is a case that dies.
Why This Matters Now

Seatbelt non-use used to be inadmissible to reduce a plaintiff's recovery in Georgia. SB 68 changed that for any lawsuit commenced on or after April 21, 2025 — regardless of when the accident happened.

Now, defendants can introduce evidence that the plaintiff wasn't wearing a seatbelt — and use it to reduce damages. This means a case worth $200,000 with a seatbelt could be worth significantly less without one. Every post-April 2025 MVA intake MUST include the seatbelt question. No exceptions.

🔍 SB 68 Seatbelt Check — Required for ALL MVAs (No Date Test at Intake)

  1. Ask the client: "Were you wearing your seatbelt at the time of the accident?" — document the answer verbatim in CasePeer
  2. If NOT wearing a seatbelt: Escalate to Managing Attorney immediately. Flag in CasePeer. Do not accept case without attorney review
  3. If wearing a seatbelt: Document confirmation. Note prominently in CasePeer
  4. Obtain police report to confirm seatbelt notation by responding officer
Do NOT apply a date test at intake: the seatbelt provision (O.C.G.A. § 40-8-76.1(d)) turns on when the lawsuit is commenced, not when the accident happened, and it reaches cases commenced on or after April 21, 2025. An older accident can still be filed today and still be exposed. Intake’s job is simple: ask every MVA client, document the answer verbatim, and flag the file for attorney review if the client was unbelted. The attorney decides whether the provision applies.

⏰ Statute of Limitations — The Absolute Deadline

Claim TypeSOLCitation
Personal Injury2 years from date of injuryO.C.G.A. § 9-3-33
Workers' Compensation1 year to file claimO.C.G.A. § 34-9-82

SOL expired = automatic decline. No exceptions, no workarounds. And when the SOL is close, flag it for IMMEDIATE attorney review — the SOL must be calendared with a named owner the same day a case is opened.

Section 5 · WC-vs-PI Classification · Up to 40 pts

Workers' Comp, Personal Injury, or Both?

"Were you injured at work, or as a civilian?" — this single question determines the case track.
The #1 Misconception

A delivery driver is rear-ended by a texting motorist while making a work delivery. The driver calls and says: "I got hurt at work — is this workers' comp?" Many intake screeners hear "hurt at work" and immediately think: WC only. Refer out.

But this is a dual-track case — the driver has BOTH a WC claim (against the employer for medical and income benefits) AND a PI claim (against the texting driver for full damages). The exclusive remedy doctrine bars suing the employer, but it never bars suing a third-party tortfeasor. Missing the PI component means leaving the most valuable part of the case on the table.

🔀 The Screening Decision Tree

Step 1: Where did the injury happen?

  • AT WORK / during work duties → Workers' compensation track (includes: on employer premises, traveling for work [not commuting], employer-provided vehicle)
  • NOT AT WORK → Personal injury track (includes: car accidents while commuting or personal use, slip & falls at stores, dog bites)

Step 2: Is there a third party involved?

  • Work injury + third-party caused it → DUAL-TRACK (WC + PI)
  • Work injury + employer/coworker only → WC ONLY (exclusive remedy)

⚖️ Exclusive Remedy — Why You Can't Sue the Employer

Workers' compensation is a trade-off: the employee gets no-fault benefits (medical + income, regardless of who caused the injury), and in exchange, the employee gives up the right to sue the employer or coworkers in civil court. This is called the exclusive remedy doctrine.

The bar protects ONLY the employer and coworkers. It never protects a third-party tortfeasor (a stranger, another company, etc.).

Current Firm Policy: GLG refers pure workers' compensation cases to WC-specialist attorneys. For dual-track cases where both WC and PI apply, always escalate to the Managing Attorney for routing guidance — the attorney determines whether GLG takes the PI side, refers the WC side, or handles both.

Section 6 · Escalation & Boundaries · Up to 40 pts

Government Entities, Communication Boundaries & Compound Screening

Some red flags have deadlines that will kill the case if you miss them. And some questions are never yours to answer.
The Deadline That Kills Cases

A caller was hit by a city garbage truck 5 months ago. The intake screener accepts the case as a standard MVA and routes it to a paralegal. Nobody flags the government entity.

The municipal ante litem notice deadline is 6 months. By the time the attorney discovers the government defendant, there are 3 weeks left to serve written notice. Scrambling begins. If that notice deadline passes without service, the case is dead — no matter how strong the liability, no matter how severe the injuries. Dead.

🏛️ Government Entity Escalation — SAME DAY

Entity TypeAnte Litem DeadlineCitation
Municipal (city, county)6 monthsO.C.G.A. § 36-33-5
State entities12 monthsO.C.G.A. § 50-21-26

Written ante litem notice must be served BEFORE suit can be filed. Miss the deadline = case dies. Escalate to Managing Attorney SAME DAY. Do not accept or decline until the attorney confirms deadline status.

🚫 Communication Boundaries — What Intake Does NOT Do

  • Does NOT provide legal advice — explain the process in general terms only
  • Does NOT promise outcomes, timelines, or settlement amounts
  • Does NOT discuss case value, liability, or strategy
  • Does NOT quote fee percentages for WC cases (Board-set fees)
  • Does NOT advise on whether someone is an employee vs. contractor
  • If a client asks a legal question: "That's a great question — I'll make sure the attorney addresses that with you."
Government Entity Indicators: City buses, county vehicles, MARTA, school buses, public works trucks, state highway patrol, federal agency vehicles, government buildings, public parks, state hospitals. When you hear ANY of these, escalate — same day.
