When Pre-Lit Becomes Litigation

Three triggers. Any one of them can move a case from negotiation to the courtroom pipeline.

⚡ The Three Litigation Triggers

#TriggerWhat It Means
1Settlement FailureThe adjuster’s best offer is unreasonable after Holt demand and multiple rounds of negotiation. Further negotiation will not produce a fair result.
2SOL PressureThe statute of limitations (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33: 2 years for PI) is approaching. Filing preserves the claim even if settlement discussions continue.
3Disputed LiabilityLiability cannot be established without discovery tools (depositions, interrogatories, document requests) that are only available in litigation.

📊 The Litigation Decision Framework

File when: Adjuster’s offer is <40% of reasonable case value after Holt demand • SOL is within 6 months • Liability is genuinely disputed and requires discovery • Multiple defendants need to be joined for full recovery

Keep negotiating when: Offer is within reasonable range and improving • Additional medical documentation may change the adjuster’s evaluation • SOL is 12+ months away • Client prefers to avoid litigation

⏱ Georgia Statute of Limitations

Personal Injury: 2 years from the date of injury (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33)

Property Damage: 4 years (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-30)

Wrongful Death: 2 years (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33)

Government entities: Ante-litem notice required first — State: 12 months; County: 12 months; City/Municipality: 6 months

Missing the SOL = malpractice. There is no court mechanism to extend the statute of limitations. If the complaint is not filed before the deadline, the client’s claim is forever barred. Calendar this deadline at intake. Verify it regularly. Treat it as the single most important date in every case file.

Section 3 · Georgia Superior Court Filing · Up to 40 pts

Filing Requirements & Service of Process

Venue, documents, fees, and proper service — every step must be right the first time.

📍 Venue — Where to File

General Rule (O.C.G.A. § 9-10-31): A suit against a Georgia-resident individual must be filed in the Superior Court of the county where the defendant resides.

Corporate defendants: May be sued where the corporation maintains a registered agent, where it transacts business, or where the cause of action arose (O.C.G.A. § 14-2-510).

Multiple defendants in different counties: File where any one defendant could properly be sued; the other defendants can be served across county lines.

📄 Filing Requirements

DocumentDetails
ComplaintThe formal pleading stating the plaintiff’s claims — must comply with O.C.G.A. § 9-11-8(a)
Civil Case Filing FormRequired information form for the clerk’s records and court administration
SummonsOne summons per defendant — issued by the clerk upon filing; directs defendant to respond within 30 days
Filing FeeTypically $200–$300 depending on the county; verify the exact amount with the clerk before filing

📬 Service of Process (O.C.G.A. § 9-11-4)

Personal service is the default method for original complaints in Georgia:

  • County Sheriff: File the summons and complaint with the sheriff’s office in the county where the defendant can be found
  • Special Process Server: The court may appoint a certified private process server
  • Corporate defendants: Serve the registered agent listed with the Georgia Secretary of State

NOT valid for original service: Certified mail from the law firm, email, fax, or regular mail. These are NOT accepted methods for initial service of an original complaint.

Service by publication is only available when the defendant cannot be found after diligent search. It requires a court order and publication in the county’s legal organ newspaper for four consecutive weeks. This is a last resort, not a default method.
Service has a clock. It starts the day the stamped complaint comes back.

Same day the stamped complaint returns: summons + complaint go to the process server. Courtesy copy out the same day service is initiated. Service initiation logged in CasePeer the same day. This is not a preference — getting the summons to the server the day the clerk returns the stamped complaint is what preserves the chance of service inside the 5-day relation-back window. A day lost here cannot be recovered later.

If the defendant is not served, the response is a written ladder — not your judgment:

  • Day 5 — Follow up with the process server. Second attempt at a different time and/or address. The relation-back window is closing.
  • Day 14 — Escalation Ladder Step 1 (substituted service). Document every prior attempt. Lead Litigation Attorney review.
  • Day 30 — Steps 1–2 exhausted. Move to Steps 3–4. Written status update in CasePeer. Lead Litigation Attorney escalation.

Do not improvise a service strategy. The ladder is written. The full ladder is in the Service of Process SOP §3; defendant-type-specific tracks (individual / entity / nonresident) are in the Service Chain Decision Tree. If your situation is not on the ladder, that is a Stop and Ask, not a decision you make alone.

Firm standard: no case goes 14 days without a documented service attempt or status update. Not “no news” — a documented attempt or a documented status. Every service action is logged in CasePeer the same day with date · method · result · next step planned with a specific date · server details. A next step without a date is not a next step.

Unserved defendants = stalled case.

Section 4 · Complaint Drafting Essentials · Up to 40 pts

What Goes in a Georgia PI Complaint

Notice pleading under O.C.G.A. § 9-11-8(a) — the complaint’s job is notice, not proof.

📝 Georgia Notice Pleading Standard

O.C.G.A. § 9-11-8(a): A complaint must contain “a short and plain statement of the claims showing that the pleader is entitled to relief” and “a demand for judgment for the relief to which the pleader deems himself entitled.”

This is notice pleading — the complaint gives the defendant fair notice of the claims. Detailed evidence comes during discovery.

📋 Anatomy of a PI Complaint

SectionContent
CaptionCourt name, plaintiff name, defendant name(s), case number (assigned by clerk)
Jurisdiction & VenueCite O.C.G.A. § 9-10-31; identify the county and why it is proper
Party AllegationsFull legal names, residences, and/or registered agents for each party
Factual AllegationsDate, time, location, and circumstances of the accident
Legal TheoriesNegligence (duty, breach, causation, damages); plus any additional theories (negligence per se, respondeat superior, etc.)
DamagesPhysical injuries, pain and suffering, medical expenses, lost wages, future damages
Prayer for ReliefSpecific relief sought (compensatory damages, punitive damages if applicable, costs of action)
Verification & SignatureAttorney signature with Georgia Bar number

⚖️ Common Legal Theories in PI Complaints

  • Negligence: Duty + Breach + Causation + Damages — the foundation of every PI claim
  • Negligence per se: Defendant violated a statute designed to protect people like the plaintiff (e.g., traffic laws under O.C.G.A. § 40-6-20) — creates a presumption of negligence
  • Respondeat superior: Employer is liable for employee’s negligence committed within the scope of employment — critical in trucking cases
  • Punitive damages: O.C.G.A. § 51-12-5.1 — available when defendant showed willful misconduct, malice, fraud, or wanton disregard for safety
Do NOT include confidential settlement communications. The complaint is a public document. Prior settlement demands, offer amounts, and negotiation details should NEVER appear in the complaint. Keep settlement history separate from pleadings.

Deadlines, Defaults & Calendar Discipline

A missed deadline is not an inconvenience. It is a case-ending event or a malpractice claim.

⏰ Critical Litigation Deadlines

DeadlineRuleConsequence of Missing
SOL — 2 yearsO.C.G.A. § 9-3-33Claim is forever barred. Malpractice liability.
Answer — 30 daysO.C.G.A. § 9-11-12(a)Plaintiff may file for default judgment (O.C.G.A. § 9-11-55).
Discovery DeadlinePer scheduling orderEvidence may be excluded; sanctions possible.
Scheduling OrderCourt-issuedAll dates in the order are enforceable; missed dates require motion to modify.

📐 The 30-Day Answer Period

Under O.C.G.A. § 9-11-12(a), the defendant has 30 days from the date of service to file an answer or responsive pleading.

If the defendant fails to answer: The plaintiff may file a motion for default judgment under O.C.G.A. § 9-11-55. Default means the defendant loses by failing to respond — not because the case was decided on the merits.

Staff responsibility: Calendar the answer deadline the moment service is confirmed. Set a 7-day advance warning. Monitor the court docket for the defendant’s filing. Alert the attorney immediately if the deadline passes without an answer.

📋 Calendar Management Best Practices

  • Redundancy: Staff enters deadlines in CasePeer; attorney verifies; advance warnings set; specific person assigned to track
  • All deadlines, not just the next one: Calendar the answer deadline, discovery cutoff, scheduling order dates, mediation date, and trial date — all at once
  • Proactive monitoring: Check docket BEFORE the deadline, not on the deadline day
  • CasePeer task system: Each deadline becomes a task assigned to a specific person with due date and advance warning
Single-point-of-failure prevention: If only one person tracks deadlines and they are sick, on vacation, or overwhelmed, the deadline is missed. Redundancy (staff + attorney + CasePeer alerts) prevents malpractice.

File Audit, Document Management & Attorney-Staff Roles

Litigation requires airtight documentation. Every document is potential evidence.

📂 CasePeer Status Flow for Litigation

Pre-Lit Statuses → Pending Litigation → Filed → Service → Discovery → Mediation → Trial

Each status change triggers new responsibilities. “Pending Litigation” means complaint preparation. “Filed” means the complaint is with the court. “Service” means the defendant has been served and the answer clock is running.

🔍 The Pre-Filing File Audit

Before drafting the complaint, conduct a complete file audit:

  • All medical records — current and complete through most recent treatment
  • Police report — in file and reviewed
  • All pre-lit correspondence — demand letters, adjuster responses, insurance communications
  • Photos and evidence — accident scene, vehicle damage, injuries
  • Witness statements — documented and organized
  • Insurance information — all policies, coverage limits, UM/UIM details
  • Client intake documents — signed retainer, HIPAA authorizations, CYA advisement letter

Gaps discovered after filing create discovery problems. Find and fix gaps before the complaint.

👥 Attorney-Staff Coordination in Litigation

TaskStaff RoleAttorney Role
Complaint preparationGather facts, assemble case file, draft initial sectionsReview, finalize legal theories, sign and file
Filing logisticsPrepare filing form, summons, payment; coordinate with clerkVerify venue and filing requirements
Service trackingDeliver to sheriff/process server; track attempts; log in CasePeerEscalate if service cannot be perfected
Deadline managementEnter ALL deadlines; set warnings; monitor docketVerify deadlines; make strategic decisions
Discovery responsesGather responsive documents; organize by request number; prepare indexReview; privilege determinations; draft objections; finalize
Discovery preparation is a team effort. Staff gathers and organizes documents. The attorney makes privilege calls, drafts objections, and finalizes responses. Never draft discovery responses independently or forward requests to clients without attorney guidance.
Document everything in CasePeer. Every phone call, email, filing, service attempt, and deadline should be logged with date, time, and details. If it is not in CasePeer, it did not happen. Complete documentation protects the firm, the attorney, and the client.
