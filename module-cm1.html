Section 2 · WHY Treatment Monitoring Matters · Up to 40 pts

The Gap Destroyer: Case Value, Defense Strategy & the 14-Day Rule

Treatment compliance is not a soft skill. It is the #1 variable in case value - and every gap is a weapon the defense gets to use.

📊 The Dollar Impact of Treatment Gaps

ScenarioTreatment RecordAdjuster OfferOutcome
Client AConsistent. 24 sessions, 0 gaps. Medical discharge on file.$52,000Full case value realized
Client B21-day gap, Weeks 5-7. No explanation documented. Resumed but inconsistent.$28,000$24,000 destroyed by one gap

⚠️ How Defense Uses Treatment Gaps

The defense playbook for treatment gaps is simple and devastatingly effective:

  1. Deposition weapon: "You stopped going to physical therapy for 19 days in November - did the pain improve during that time?" The implied answer: yes, you were getting better, therefore the injury was minor.
  2. Comparative fault argument: Under Georgia's modified comparative fault standard, failing to follow prescribed treatment can be framed as failure to mitigate damages - assigning a percentage of "fault" to the client that reduces their recovery.
  3. Adjuster pricing: Gaps are visible in medical records. Adjusters are trained to price gaps as signal of recovery - "if you were really hurt, you would have kept going."
The 14-Day Rule: A 14-day gap in treatment records is the threshold at which insurance carriers begin arguing the injury has healed. The CM's job is to prevent this gap from ever forming through proactive contact - not to react after the gap already exists.
Georgia's modified comparative fault rule: If a client is found 50% or more at fault for their own damages - including failure to mitigate by not following treatment - they recover nothing. Every gap is potential comparative fault ammunition.

📌 Where Treatment Data Lives in CasePeer

There is no single dashboard or automated flag system. CMs track treatment across three sources:

  • Medical records on file - Provider visit documentation uploaded to the case. This is the primary source for actual visit dates and treatment progress.
  • Case notes - The CM's treatment update logs from every check-in call. These document last visit date, next appointment, client-reported status, and any compliance concerns.
  • Injuries section - Injury details, treating providers, and provider specialties. This is where you see who the client is treating with and for what.

The CM's daily workflow: sort active cases by last contact date in your case notes, identify any cases approaching the 14-day threshold, and prioritize those for outreach.

🗒️ The Treatment Timeline: Expected vs. Actual

Every treatment plan has a schedule. CMs track two numbers against each other:

  • Expected sessions - what the provider ordered (e.g., 3x/week × 8 weeks = 24 sessions)
  • Actual sessions - what CasePeer shows as documented visits

When actual falls more than 2 sessions behind expected: treat it as urgent and contact the client that day. Do not wait for the 14-day calendar trigger.

Documentation Rule: Every treatment check-in - even a two-minute call where the client says they're doing fine - must be logged in CasePeer before the CM moves to the next case. Date, time, what the client said, next contact date. No exceptions.

Section 4 · The Treatment Conversation · Up to 40 pts

Check-In Calls, Non-Compliance Conversations & Calibrated Questions

How you ask matters as much as what you ask. Clients who feel interrogated stop answering. Clients who feel supported keep going to treatment.

📞 The Check-In Call Framework

  1. Open with empathy - "How are you feeling this week? Is the treatment helping?" Information after connection. A client who feels heard answers honestly.
  2. Confirm treatment - Specific, not vague: "When was your last appointment? Do you have one scheduled this week?"
  3. Diagnose before prescribing - If something is wrong, ask why before offering solutions: "What's been making it hard to get to your appointments lately?"
  4. Reference prior commitments - "Last time we spoke, you mentioned Tuesday and Thursday - how did those go?" Not accusatory; just memory-jogging. Clients who stated a commitment are more likely to follow through when reminded.
  5. Close with the next step - "So your next appointment is Wednesday at 2 PM? Great - I'll check in with you Thursday to see how it went."

⚠️ When Treatment Is Slipping: Calibrated Questions

Calibrated questions are open-ended, non-accusatory, and designed to surface the real barrier rather than provoke defensiveness. Examples:

  • "What's been making it hard to get to your appointments lately?"
  • "What would need to happen for getting there to feel easier?"
  • "I hear you that you're feeling better - what does your doctor say about where you are in the plan?"

Never say: "You have to go." "You're hurting your case." "The insurance company will use this against you." These close the conversation instead of opening it.

After Every Call - Log It: Date, time, what the client said, any compliance concern, the next scheduled appointment, and next contact date. A 2-minute positive call gets the same documentation standard as a 15-minute non-compliance conversation. The defense sees what's in CasePeer - and only what's in CasePeer.

🗒️ GLG Treatment Note Format - Required Elements

Every treatment check-in note must include all of these elements:

  • "TREATMENT UPDATE [date time]" - use this exact header
  • Contact type - phone call, text, email
  • Last visit date and provider
  • Next scheduled appointment date
  • Client-reported status - verbatim or close paraphrase of what the client said
  • CM assessment - is treatment on schedule or is there a compliance concern? Note any issues.
  • Next contact due date
Weekly Case Review Prep: Before each case review meeting, pull CasePeer reports showing all active treatment cases with last contact dates and days since last documented treatment. Review your case notes for any cases approaching or past the 14-day threshold, and any where sessions are behind the prescribed plan. Walk into the meeting knowing which cases need attorney attention.

Section 6 · Escalation Protocol · Up to 40 pts

When to Escalate, How to Document & SOL Awareness

Escalation is not failure. Escalation without documentation is.

🚨 Escalation Triggers - When the CM Must Escalate to the Attorney

TriggerActionUrgency
14-day gap - client unreachableEscalate to supervising attorney with documented contact attemptsImmediate
Client actively refusing treatmentEscalate - attorney call required; CM cannot authorize treatment discontinuationImmediate
Provider issue (stopped accepting client, billing dispute)Escalate - attorney may need to arrange alternative providerSame day
Client self-discharged without MD sign-offEscalate - attorney must evaluate whether record is sufficient for demandSame day
Any gap near SOL deadlineEscalate immediately regardless of gap length - the SOL clock does not pause for treatment issuesImmediate

📋 The Escalation Memo - Required Elements

When you escalate, give the attorney everything in one place. "Client isn't going" is not an escalation memo.

  1. Client name and case number
  2. Last treatment date and how many days ago
  3. Last CM contact date and method
  4. All contact attempts since last successful contact (date, method, outcome)
  5. Client's stated reason (if known)
  6. Specials amount at risk
  7. CM recommendation - attorney call, treatment reinstatement letter, or case evaluation
SOL Awareness: Georgia's statute of limitations for personal injury is 2 years. A treatment gap + client unreachability within 60 days of the SOL deadline is the highest-urgency combination in case management. The attorney may need to file a protective complaint before the deadline regardless of treatment status.
