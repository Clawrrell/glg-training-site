Section 2 · Demand Package Contents · Up to 40 pts

What Goes Into a Demand Package

A demand is only as strong as what supports it. Missing a record doesn't just weaken one section — it anchors the adjuster's entire valuation too low.

📁 The Six-Component Demand Structure

Per GLG's demand preparation standards, every demand letter must contain all six components. A demand missing any component is incomplete and will be returned for revision.

⚠️ READ THIS BEFORE THE LIST BELOW — two different six-item lists exist, and they are NOT the same list.

The six components below are a drafting and persuasion structure. They are GLG's internal standard for how you write a demand that reads as a legal argument instead of an invoice.

Separately, O.C.G.A. § 9-11-67.1 sets out the material terms the law requires for a pre-suit time-limited settlement offer in a motor vehicle case to carry statutory effect. That is a compliance checklist with legal consequences.

Satisfying the six components below does NOT make a demand statutorily compliant. Satisfying the statute does NOT make a demand persuasive. They are checked separately, and both must be checked. A demand can be perfectly structured and still fail the statute — and a CM who assumes otherwise can destroy the client's time-limited-demand leverage.
  1. Facts of Loss — police report, photos/video evidence, witness statements, officer citations, scene reconstruction if applicable. Establishes liability from the first sentence.
  2. Determination of Liability and Causation — torts analysis connecting the defendant's breach of duty directly to the client's specific injuries. Not just "they hit us" — but "their violation of [rule] caused [specific harm] through [mechanism]."
  3. Bodily Injuries and Pain & Suffering — full treatment timeline, every provider's findings, MRI/imaging results, surgeon recommendations, functional limitations, and the client's lived experience of pain, daily impact, and psychological harm.
  4. Summary of Expenses and Medical Bills — full ledger using total billed amounts (not health insurance adjusted or paid amounts). See inline primer below.
  5. Additional "Heat" — punitive factors (reckless behavior, aggravating circumstances), Georgia verdict/settlement data from Case-Metrix for comparable injuries, before/after affidavits documenting life impact. Pushes demand value above raw specials.
  6. Demand for Payment with Conditions — the specific dollar amount demanded and the conditions attached to it (deadline, how payment is to be delivered, what will be released). Where the demand is a pre-suit time-limited offer in a motor vehicle case, the conditions stated here must independently satisfy every material term in O.C.G.A. § 9-11-67.1 — see the separate statutory primer below. Writing this component does not, by itself, achieve statutory compliance.
Inline Primer — Total Billed Amounts: Medical bills in the demand must reflect the gross amount billed by the provider — not the amount paid by health insurance, not the adjusted/negotiated rate, not the client's out-of-pocket. Example: Hospital bills $48,000. Health insurance pays $18,000 and writes off $22,000. The demand shows $48,000. This is the starting anchor. If health insurance has a subrogation claim, that is handled in the post-settlement phase — not in the demand.
Inline Primer — O.C.G.A. § 9-11-67.1 (the STATUTORY list — separate from the six components above):

This statute governs a pre-suit time-limited offer to settle a claim for personal injury, bodily injury, or death arising from a motor vehicle collision, made before all named defendants have answered or are in default, and prepared by or with the assistance of an attorney. It does not apply to every demand GLG sends — check the case type first.

Subsection (b)(1) states these are the material terms, and that they are the ONLY material terms. There are seven, lettered (A)–(G):

(A) A date by which the offer must be accepted — not less than 30 days from receipt of the offer sent by certified mail or statutory overnight delivery, return receipt requested.
(B) Amount of monetary payment.
(C) The party or parties the claimant will release if the offer is accepted.
(D) For any type of release, whether the release is full or limited, and an itemization of what the claimant will provide to each releasee.
(E) The claims to be released.
(F) A date by which payment must be delivered — not less than 40 days from receipt of the offer.
(G) A requirement that, to settle, the recipient provide a statement under oath as to whether all liability and casualty coverage that provides or may provide coverage has been disclosed, and a date for delivery of that statement — not less than 40 days from receipt. This term may be waived by the offeror.

Any other term is an immaterial term under subsection (c) — the recipient varying from it does not defeat acceptance. No party may require another to waive or modify the application of this Code section.

⚠️ Note what is NOT on this list: "all records and bills attached" and "a statement that the offer expires" are not statutory material terms. They are good practice — attaching supporting material is how the adjuster evaluates the claim — but do not confuse them with the statute's requirements. Equally, the six drafting components above (Facts of Loss, Additional Heat, and so on) appear nowhere in this statute.

Escalate to the attorney of record for any time-limited demand. The CM does not make the compliance call.

Section 3 · The Demand Narrative · Up to 40 pts

Structure, Tone & What the Demand Argues

The demand narrative is not a summary — it is a legal argument. It sets the anchor, frames the liability, and tells the adjuster what this case is worth before they open a single record.

📝 The Demand Narrative — Purpose and Tone

The demand narrative is the persuasive core of the demand package. It does three things: (1) It argues — connecting the defendant's rule violation to the client's injuries through a causal chain; (2) It anchors — setting the opening dollar amount that defines the negotiation range; (3) It pressures — making clear the firm knows the law, knows the value, and is ready to litigate if the carrier does not engage seriously.

Tone: Measured, professional, and confident — not emotional, not threatening. The narrative reads like a brief, not a complaint letter. It leads with facts and arguments, not with the client's feelings.

🦎 The Reptile-Informed Demand Sequence

GLG's demand narratives follow the safety-rule framework. The sequence activates the adjuster's awareness that their insured broke a safety rule — and that paying is how the community enforces those rules.

  1. Safety Rule Violation — identify the specific rule the defendant broke (e.g., "A driver must maintain a safe following distance to prevent rear-end collisions"). State the violation plainly.
  2. Causation Chain — connect the rule violation to the specific injuries through the mechanism: how the force traveled from vehicle to body, what it did inside the body (mechanism of wreck + mechanism of harm).
  3. Bodily Injuries and Life Impact — full treatment timeline, objective findings (MRI, surgical recommendations), functional limitations, pain, psychological harm, daily life disruption.
  4. Damages — total billed medical expenses, lost wages, pain and suffering multiple, future care projections (if applicable).
  5. Additional Heat — verdict tables, punitive factors, before/after affidavits. These push the demand above raw specials and signal that the firm has done the same research an expert witness would.
Inline Primer — Mechanism of Wreck + Mechanism of Harm (MIST cases): In soft-tissue/MIST cases, the adjuster's strategy is to minimize impact severity ("it was just a fender-bender") to minimize injury severity. The demand narrative must counter this by explaining: (1) Mechanism of Wreck — how even a moderate-speed impact transmits significant force through the vehicle frame directly to the occupant's body; (2) Mechanism of Harm — step by step, what that force does inside the body: it straightens the cervical spine's natural curve, snaps the head in whiplash motion, tears ligaments and fascia, and compresses discs. These explanations must come from the treating physician — never from the CM's own characterization.
What the Narrative Must NOT Do: It cannot quote dollar amounts the CM thinks the case is worth — value belongs to the attorney. It cannot characterize client credibility — that is attorney judgment. It cannot speculate about future treatment beyond what the treating physician documented. Every fact in the narrative must be sourced to a document in the package.

Templates #08, #09, #24, #32 — and the Demand Clock

Four templates. Four distinct contexts. The wrong template at the wrong stage wastes leverage and creates legal exposure. The right template at the right time is a systems choice, not an improvised one.

📄 The Four Demand-Stage Templates

TemplateNameWhen UsedAuthorization
#08Demand_Package_LetterThe cover/transmittal letter sent to the adjuster enclosing all demand materials — introduces the demand, identifies the case, and encloses the narrative + recordsCM prepares; attorney approves
#09Demand_NarrativeThe substantive legal argument document — all six components, the primary persuasive content of the packageCM drafts; attorney reviews and approves
#24Policy_Limits_DemandWhen documented damages clearly exceed the at-fault carrier's BI policy limits — formally demands payment of the full policy limit; creates bad faith exposure if carrier fails to pay limits when they shouldCM prepares on attorney direction
#32Time_Limited_Demand_HoltO.C.G.A. § 9-11-67.1 compliant pre-suit time-limited demand (motor vehicle claims) — acceptance deadline not less than 30 days from receipt; must contain all seven statutory material terms (A)–(G), which are separate from the six drafting componentsAttorney authorization REQUIRED — every time. No exceptions.

⏱ The Demand Timeline — When Demands Must Go Out

MilestoneDeadline / RuleConsequence of Missing
Client reaches MMITarget: demand within 30 days of MMI (maximum medical improvement — treating physician confirms no further improvement expected)Demand understates total damages; adjuster prices as a disorganized file
Day 30 post-MMIDemand should be out or escalate to attorney for extension authorizationLeverage lost; adjuster's reserve hasn't been set yet; urgency signal weakens
Day 45 post-DemandedIf no adjuster response in 45 days, follow-up action required immediately. No exceptions. No waiting.Adjuster prices delay as low urgency; negotiating position weakens without a live clock
Demand sent too earlyNever send demand before client reaches MMI — future treatment bills will arrive after the demand and weaken the anchorDemand understates total damages; impossible to add bills post-demand credibly
Inline Primer — MMI (Maximum Medical Improvement): MMI is the point at which the treating physician certifies that the client has recovered as much as medically possible. It does not mean the client is fully recovered — it means no further significant improvement is expected. A demand sent before MMI is premature: future treatment, future bills, and the full extent of permanent injury are not yet documented. The demand anchor is only as high as the documented damages at the time it is sent.
Template #32 Authorization Rule: A CM may never prepare or send Template #32 (Holt demand) without explicit written attorney authorization — on every case, every time. The Holt demand creates specific legal obligations and deadlines. A defective Holt, or a Holt sent without authorization, is a malpractice risk. "The attorney will approve it later" is not authorization.

Section 5 · Quality Checklist & Attorney vs. CM Roles · Up to 40 pts

Pre-Submission Checklist & Role Delineation

The CM assembles and preflights. The attorney reviews and approves. The attorney sends — or explicitly authorizes the CM to send. These roles never reverse.

⚖️ Attorney vs. CM — Role Ownership

CM OwnsAttorney Owns
Gathering all records and bills from every providerReviewing the demand narrative for legal accuracy and argument quality
Drafting Template #09 (Demand Narrative) for attorney reviewApproving the demand package before it goes to the adjuster
Preparing Template #08 (Demand Package Letter)Setting the demand dollar amount — CMs do not decide what to demand
Running the pre-submission quality checklistSending the demand — or explicitly authorizing the CM to send
Advancing CasePeer status through the demand workflowAuthorizing Template #24 (Policy Limits) and Template #32 (Holt)
CM CANNOT DO without attorney direction: Set the demand dollar amount · Send the demand · Prepare or send Template #24 or #32 · Advance status to 'Demanded' without attorney authorization

✅ Pre-Submission Quality Checklist — All Items Required

The CM completes this checklist BEFORE submitting to the attorney. A package submitted with missing items wastes attorney review time and delays the demand.

  1. All medical records present — full chart notes (not just billing statements) from every accident-related treating provider
  2. All medical bills present and itemized — total billed amounts confirmed for each provider; grand total calculated correctly
  3. Police/accident report included — with any citations, diagrams, and officer narrative
  4. Photos and/or video attached (if applicable) — vehicle damage, scene, injuries
  5. Lost wages documentation included (if applicable) — employer letter confirming wages and weeks missed
  6. Demand narrative (Template #09) complete — all six components present; proofread; no unsourced facts
  7. CasePeer status advanced to 'Demand Prepped' — signals to the attorney that the package is ready for review
Demand Prepped ≠ Demand Sent: Advancing CasePeer status to 'Demand Prepped' means the CM has completed assembly and the package is ready for attorney review — not that it has been sent. The attorney reviews, approves, and then either sends the demand or explicitly authorizes the CM to send. 'Demanded' status is set only after the demand has actually been transmitted to the adjuster.

The Five Demand Errors & Status Documentation

Every demand error the CM commits becomes the adjuster's argument. The most dangerous errors don't show up until the first offer — which is always lower than it should be.

🚫 Five Demand Errors That Weaken or Delay Every Case

#ErrorWhat It Costs
1Sending before MMI — demand goes out while client is still treatingFuture bills not captured; demand anchor is permanently set too low; can't credibly add records post-demand
2Incomplete records — missing providers, billing statements without chart notes, or missing diagnostic studiesAdjuster evaluates only what they received; low offer anchored to incomplete specials; recovering from low anchor is harder than never setting one
3Invoice narrative — demand reads as a bill summary instead of a legal argument; no safety rule, no causation chain, no Additional HeatAdjuster sees no litigation risk; values the file as the invoice says; negotiation range collapses
4Unexplained treatment gaps — demand ignores gaps the adjuster will certainly findAdjuster uses gap to cut value; CM has no documented rebuttal; attorney must reconstruct the explanation after the fact
5Using adjusted/paid amounts instead of total billed — medical bills show what health insurance paid, not what was billedDemand understates specials; adjuster calculates their offer as a multiple of the lower billed amount; lost value cannot be recovered post-send

The 45-Day Clock Starts at Demanded: The moment the status advances to 'Demanded' and the certified mail tracking number is logged, the 45-day clock begins. The CM is responsible for monitoring this clock and triggering follow-up before Day 45 passes. This is a logged deadline — not a mental note.
