Team Leadership & CM Oversight

Every case outcome in pre-lit is yours. Not because you handled it — because your team did, under your leadership.
PA Track — Module 6

Last month, a client called Harrell directly. She said nobody from the firm had contacted her in five weeks.

The assigned CM had fallen behind on her caseload. Her callback queue had three items older than 48 hours. Communication compliance for the month was at 38%. She had stopped logging contact attempts in CasePeer. Nobody caught it. Not during the weekly huddle. Not during the case file review. Not until the client — scared, frustrated, and feeling abandoned — escalated past the entire department to the managing attorney. The failure was not one CM dropping the ball. The failure was a supervision system that allowed five weeks of silence to go undetected.

Section 2 · The Leadership Mindset · Up to 40 pts

Owning Every Outcome

The department head does not own a caseload. The department head owns every caseload.
The Shift

You were the best case evaluator in the firm. Now your job is to build a team of case evaluators.

As a senior attorney, your value was measured by YOUR case outcomes. As department head, your value is measured by the consistency and quality of every case outcome across the entire pre-lit department. A department where one CM produces excellent demands and three produce mediocre ones is not a department with one good CM — it is a department with a leadership gap that allows inconsistency. Your oversight system is what makes the difference.

💡 The Five Pillars of a High-Performing Team

A pre-lit department that produces consistent results is built on these foundations:

1Trust: Not politeness-based ("we all get along") but vulnerability-based ("I can admit mistakes and ask for help without fear"). When you go first on admitting your own errors, you signal that honesty is safe.
2Productive Conflict: Debate about cases, strategies, and standards — not personal attacks. If every huddle is harmonious but problems persist, that is artificial harmony. Real issues are not being surfaced.
3Commitment: Clarity plus buy-in, not consensus. Every huddle ends with specific commitments: who does what by when. General aspirations are not commitments.
4Mutual Accountability: Peer-to-peer, not just top-down. If a CM sees a colleague missing the 14-day standard and says nothing, that is not collegiality — it is dysfunction. The team should expect the standard from each other.
5Results Focus: Team outcomes over individual metrics. A CM with a perfect individual scorecard who refuses to help a struggling colleague is not a team player — they are a silo.

Section 3 · Case Review & Quality Control · Up to 40 pts

The Oversight Rhythm That Runs Without Heroics

Ad-hoc reviews when you have time are not a system. A predictable cadence that runs whether you are busy or not — that is a system.
The Demand That Should Have Been Caught

A CM submitted a $52K demand on a surgical case with $45K in specials. No safety rule framing. No mechanism of harm. No community danger language.

The adjuster countered at $28K. The CM accepted without attorney consultation. The case settled for approximately 60% of its actual value. When the file was reviewed two months later, the demand was a three-page summary of medical bills with a dollar amount at the bottom. No structured review process existed. The demand was never seen by an attorney before it went out. The CM did not know it was deficient because nobody had ever shown them what a strong demand looks like.

📅 The Structured Review Cadence

Review TypeFrequencyWhat You Review
Weekly PITT ReviewEvery week, per CMActive case development: treatment status, records status, demand timeline, blockers. 20–30 min per CM. Calendar-blocked. Non-negotiable.
Demand ReviewEvery demand, before submissionSafety rule checklist, valuation, mechanism of harm, community framing, settlement posture. NO demand goes to an adjuster without your sign-off.
Settlement AuthorityPer case, as neededAll settlements. CMs are NOT authorized to accept offers without attorney consultation. Cases exceeding $50K require your direct review of settlement posture.
Case File AuditMonthly spot-checkRandom sample of case files: documentation quality, CasePeer entries, task completion, communication logs.
Stagnant Case ReviewWeekly (as part of huddle)Cases past 90 days without demand readiness. Any case on this list must have an action plan with a deadline.

✅ The Demand Evaluation Checklist

Per the Pre-Lit Head Attorney SOP, every demand you review must pass these five questions before submission:

1Safety rule violation identified and stated? A demand without a clear safety rule violation cannot anchor an aggressive settlement position.
2Mechanism of harm explained? How force traveled from vehicle to body. The adjuster needs to see the physics before they see the bills.
3Community interest established? Not just this plaintiff — the safety rule protects everyone. "Anyone could have been at that intersection."
4"Random victim" framing applied? Could anyone have been hurt by this defendant's conduct? If yes, the jury cares about enforcement, not just compensation.
5Demand value anchored appropriately? High enough to allow room for negotiation while serving the client. Never anchor low.
Demand Review Turnaround: Per the SOP performance standards, your demand review turnaround must be within 48 hours of CM submission. If a CM submits a demand and waits a week for your review, the bottleneck is YOU. Calendar-block demand review time daily so they never stack up.
"Verbal Policy = No Policy": If the review cadence is not documented and calendared, it does not exist. A commitment to "review demands when they come in" is not a system. A calendar block that says "Demand Review: 10–11 AM daily" with a CasePeer checklist — that is a system.

The Numbers That Tell You If the System Is Working

If you wait for client complaints to tell you the team is falling behind, you have already failed. The metrics tell you first.
The Number That Should Terrify You

Firm-wide communication compliance was recently measured at 26%.

That means only 26% of pre-lit clients received contact within the required 14-day cycle. The target is 95%+. The gap is not a rounding error — it is a systemic failure. Three out of four clients are going longer than two weeks without hearing from the firm. Each one of them is a potential bar complaint, a negative review, or a lost referral. Your job is to close this gap.

📈 The Four KPIs You Own

KPIStandardWhy It Matters
Communication Compliance95%+ of clients contacted every 14 daysCurrently at 26%. This is the #1 driver of client complaints and bar risk. Moving this metric is your most urgent leadership priority.
Callback Response Rate100% within 24 hours — zero exceptionsAny callback older than 24 hours escalates to Harrell. No waiting, no explanations. An aging callback is a signal the system needs intervention NOW.
Demand QualityEvery demand passes the 5-point checklistDemand quality is the #1 lever for case value. A weak demand anchors a weak negotiation. A strong demand anchors a strong one.
Cases Past 90 DaysZero stagnant without documented action planCases stagnant beyond 90 days without demand readiness get reviewed by you. If they are sitting without a plan, that is a supervision failure.

📄 Weekly Huddle Agenda (Per SOP)

Led by you every week. Attendance mandatory for all pre-lit CMs. 20–30 minutes.

  • Demands sent last week vs. weekly target (18–20)
  • Pending demands > 60 days — review list, assign action items
  • Client complaint log — any new complaints, resolution status
  • Records bottleneck list — outstanding records blocking demand readiness
  • Post-settlement aging — files open > 45 days post-settlement
  • Individual CM flags — any cases needing attorney review
The Pulse vs. The Review: A weekly pulse check catches fires (callback queue, communication gaps, demand pipeline). A monthly KPI review analyzes trends (is demand quality improving? Are complaints declining?). You need both. The pulse catches the symptom; the monthly review diagnoses the disease.
The 24-Hour Callback Standard: Per the Communication Logging Standards SOP, any incoming call, voicemail, or message from a client or adjuster must receive a response within 24 hours on business days. Messages received after 5 PM get a response by noon the following business day. Callbacks older than 24 hours escalate to Harrell — no waiting, no exceptions. This is one of three firm vital signs.

Section 5 · Coaching & Development · Up to 40 pts

Building CMs Who Can Think, Not Just Follow

The goal of every case review is not just to check the box. The goal is to build a CM who can make the right call when you are not in the room.
The Question That Changes Everything

"What should I do?" is the most dangerous question a CM can ask you.

Not because the question is wrong — but because the easy answer is to just tell them. You know the answer. You can give it in 30 seconds. And then the CM learns nothing except to ask you again next time. The experienced attorney's instinct is to jump straight to the solution. The effective leader's discipline is to stay curious longer. Ask before telling. Diagnose before prescribing. Every time you give an answer you could have drawn out with a question, you build a team that depends on you instead of a team that can think.

💬 The Coaching Conversation in Case Reviews

Transform PITT reviews from compliance checks into development opportunities with these questions:

1"What's on your mind with this case?" — Opens the conversation beyond the checklist. Lets the CM surface what they are actually thinking about, not just what the review template asks.
2"And what else?" — The most powerful follow-up. Prevents surface-level reviews. The first answer is rarely the real issue.
3"What's the real challenge here for you?" — Focuses on the core problem. Is the CM stuck on a legal question? A client management issue? A provider who is not cooperating? Diagnose before prescribing.
4"What have you tried so far?" — Tests whether the CM has engaged with the problem or is asking you to solve it for them. Build the expectation that CMs come prepared with at least one proposed solution.
5"What would you do if I were not available?" — Forces independent thinking. The answer often reveals that the CM knows more than they think they do.

💪 The Feedback Framework: Care AND Challenge

Effective leadership requires BOTH caring personally about your team AND challenging them directly. Four quadrants:

QuadrantDescriptionResult
Care + ChallengeYou genuinely care about the person AND give honest, direct feedbackGrowth. The CM improves because they trust your intent.
Care, No ChallengeYou care but avoid difficult conversations to protect feelingsStagnation. Nice relationships but no improvement. Problems fester.
Challenge, No CareYou give blunt feedback without any personal investmentResentment. Technically correct but relationally destructive. CMs stop listening.
No Care, No ChallengeDisengaged leadership — neither honest nor investedCollapse. Team drifts without direction or development.
Coaching vs. Directing: Coaching builds the CM's judgment ("What do you think the best approach is?"). Directing tells them what to do ("Send the records request to Dr. Martin today"). Both have a place: coach on strategy and judgment, direct on compliance and deadlines. The mistake is defaulting to directing on everything — it is faster but it produces a team that cannot function without you.
Training Integration — Role Boundaries: Marisol is the firm's Training & Mentorship Lead. Skill-building sessions and competency gates are her domain. Legal quality oversight and case review coaching are yours. Clear swim lanes prevent overlap and dropped balls. When a CM has a development need, coordinate with Marisol on who handles which aspect.

Section 6 · Accountability & Role Boundaries · Up to 40 pts

The Difficult Conversation & Knowing Your Lane

Accountability without care is cruelty. Care without accountability is neglect. Your job is to do both.
The Conversation Nobody Wants to Have

A CM has been underperforming for six weeks. You have coached her twice. Nothing has changed.

Her communication compliance is at 40%. Two client complaints this month. Demand quality has slipped — the last three required significant revision. She is clearly struggling. You care about her — she is a good person who is trying hard. But caring about someone does not mean protecting them from the truth. The clients who are not getting called deserve better. The team that is picking up her slack deserves better. The hardest conversations are the most important ones. Avoiding them does not make you kind — it makes you complicit in the failure.

🔍 Root Cause Diagnosis: Three Possible Gaps

Before choosing your intervention, diagnose the root cause. The wrong fix for the wrong cause wastes time and demoralizes the CM.

Gap TypeDescriptionCorrect Intervention
Skill GapThe CM does not know HOW to do what is required. They lack training, knowledge, or experience.Training, coaching, mentoring. Work with Marisol on skill-building. Provide examples and practice opportunities.
Will GapThe CM KNOWS how but is not doing it. They have the capability but not the motivation or discipline.Direct accountability conversation. Clear expectations. Documented consequences. "What is getting in the way?"
System GapThe process itself is making success harder than it should be. Tools, workload, unclear expectations.Process redesign. Workload rebalancing. Clearer standards. Fix the system before blaming the person.

💬 The Performance Conversation Structure

  1. Start with purpose: "I want to talk about this because I care about your success here and I need to be honest with you."
  2. Share facts, not judgments: "In the last month, communication compliance was 40%, two clients filed complaints, and three demands required significant revision." Not: "You have been doing a terrible job."
  3. Make it safe: Separate the person from the behavior. "This is not about whether you are a good person or a hard worker — I know you are both. This is about specific performance standards that are not being met."
  4. Diagnose together: "Help me understand what is happening. Is this a skill issue, a workload issue, or something else?"
  5. Commit to a plan: Specific actions, specific deadlines, specific check-ins. "I want to see communication compliance at 80%+ by end of next month. We will check in weekly."
  6. Document: Date, what was discussed, commitments made, timeline. If it is not documented, the conversation did not happen.

Escalation to Harrell — When: Per the Chain of Command SOP, these require Harrell's involvement: (1) Any Tier 3 client complaint (bar complaint threats, malpractice allegations). (2) Compensation, discipline, or termination decisions. (3) Government entity cases. (4) Cases with policy limits above $500K. (5) Staffing or conduct issues that impact operations. (6) Ethical concerns or potential violations. Do NOT bypass Harrell on these. Do NOT send him routine operational issues that are within your authority.
UPL Prevention Reminder: CMs must NEVER cross the line into legal advice, case valuation, settlement authority, or strategic decisions. A CM who accepts a settlement offer without attorney consultation has committed an act of unauthorized practice of law. Your supervision system must build the habits and judgment that keep CMs safely inside the line. GA Bar Rule 5.3 makes this YOUR personal ethical obligation.
