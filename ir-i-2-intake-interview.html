Section 2 · The Structured Interview Flow · Up to 40 pts

Rapport, Sequence & the Franchise Standard

The intake interview is a conversation, not a form. But it follows a deliberate structure every single time.

📞 The Interview Sequence (Every Call, Same Order)

  1. Rapport — acknowledge what happened, reassure them, set expectations for the call
  2. Client information — name, DOB, contact info, employer, referral source (easy, non-threatening)
  3. Incident details — date, time, location, description in their own words (they want to tell this story)
  4. Insurance information — their carrier, at-fault carrier, UM/UIM, health insurance (complex, may need prompting)
  5. Medical information — injuries, ER visit, treating physician, prior injuries (sensitive)
  6. Documents — what you need from them: photos, police report, insurance card (action items)
  7. Next steps — what happens now, when they'll hear from someone, who to contact
Why this order matters: The sequence builds from comfortable to complex. You start with easy questions (name, phone number) that build momentum. By the time you reach insurance and medical questions — which callers often find confusing or sensitive — they're already engaged and trusting you. Skipping ahead to insurance or medical questions before building rapport creates resistance and incomplete answers.

🤝 The Empathy Standard

Clients calling after an accident are often in pain, scared, or frustrated. The SOP is clear: "Be empathetic — clients are often in pain, scared, or frustrated." The interview should feel like talking to a competent professional who cares, not filling out a government form. Use the caller's name. Listen before you ask the next question. Acknowledge their situation before moving to data collection.

Section 3 · Required Information · Up to 40 pts

Every Field, Every Time — The Complete Checklist

If it's on the checklist, it gets collected. No blanks. No "I'll get it later." No exceptions.
Downstream Consequence

A case manager opens a new file that was just handed off from intake. The CasePeer record shows: client name, phone number, accident date, and "neck pain." That's it. No SSN, no employer, no at-fault insurance, no UM/UIM status, no property damage info, no prior injury history, no referral source.

The case manager now has to call the client back and conduct a second interview to collect everything that should have been captured the first time. The client is annoyed — "I already told the other person everything." The insurance verification is delayed. The LOR can't be sent because there's no at-fault carrier on file. An incomplete intake doesn't just create more work — it damages the client relationship and delays every downstream step.

The Prior Injuries Question: You MUST ask about prior injuries to the same body parts. This is not optional — the SOP marks it as a required field and a red flag item. The insurance company will discover prior treatment in medical records. It's better to know on Day 1 than to be surprised 6 months into the case.

Section 4 · Insurance & CasePeer · Up to 40 pts

Insurance Collection, Case Creation & the Dual-Upload Rule

Insurance fields are where intakes fail most often. UM/UIM blank = case delayed. Single upload = non-compliant.
Real Consequence — Missed UM/UIM

A client's at-fault driver had minimum-limits coverage — $25,000. The client's medical bills alone were $38,000. Three months into the case, the paralegal discovers the client had $100,000 in UM/UIM coverage that nobody captured at intake.

That UM claim should have been opened on Day 1 with Template #02 (LOR UM) and Template #62 (UM Insurer On Notice). Instead, it was opened 90 days late. The UM carrier raised questions about the delay. The client's $100,000 in additional coverage almost went unclaimed because the intake left the UM/UIM field blank. This is why the SOP says: "No blank insurance, no blank UM/UIM, no blank property damage."

🛡️ Insurance Information — What to Collect

  • Client's auto insurance: carrier name, policy number, coverage limits
  • At-fault party's insurance: carrier name, policy number (if known)
  • UM/UIM status: Does the client have uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage? (Required — cannot be blank)
  • Health insurance: carrier name (for medical bills subrogation tracking)
  • Property damage: status and PD claim number

When callers don't know their coverage: "That's coverage that protects you if the other driver doesn't have enough insurance. It could be really important for your case. Can you check your declarations page when you get home?"

⚠️ The Dual-Upload Rule: When you receive documents, you MUST do BOTH: (1) Upload to OneDrive (in the client's folder) AND (2) Enter the information in CasePeer. If you only do one, the information is incomplete. OneDrive has the documents; CasePeer has the data. Both are needed. This is a compliance requirement, not a suggestion.

Section 5 · SOL & Documents · Up to 40 pts

SOL Calendaring, Document Collection & Case Advancement

The SOL deadline is the single most dangerous thing in a case file. Miss it, and nothing else matters.
Malpractice Scenario

A new case was opened on a Friday afternoon. The intake specialist created the CasePeer entry, uploaded the retainer, and logged off for the weekend. The SOL deadline was not calendared. The case sat in "Intake" for two weeks before being assigned. The assigned paralegal assumed intake had calendared the SOL. Nobody checked.

The statute of limitations was 18 months away — plenty of time. But 18 months passed. Treatment dragged. The demand was delayed. When the paralegal finally checked the SOL, there were 12 days left. A panicked emergency filing was required. If anyone had missed another week, the client's case would have died — and the firm would have faced a malpractice claim. All because the SOL wasn't calendared the same day the case was opened.

SOL Calendaring — Same Day, Named Owner: The statute of limitations must be calendared in CasePeer with a named owner on the SAME DAY the case is opened. Not tomorrow. Not Monday. Not "when the paralegal takes over." The standard is: Georgia PI SOL = 2 years from date of incident (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33). Failure to calendar the SOL on the day a case is opened is a corrective action trigger.

📄 Document Collection — Remote Workflow

As a remote intake specialist, all document collection happens digitally:

  • Retainer agreement: Send via DocuSign for electronic signature
  • Medical authorization / HIPAA release: Send via DocuSign
  • Police report authorization: Send via DocuSign or email for signature
  • Photos: Client emails or uploads via secure portal
  • Insurance declarations page: Client emails a copy or photo

All received documents → dual-upload (OneDrive + CasePeer). Track what's outstanding. Follow up.

Section 6 · Follow-Up & Communication · Up to 40 pts

Communication Boundaries, Remote Workflow & the No-Blanks Standard

Collect facts. Don't give advice. Document everything. Leave no field empty.
Boundary Violation

During an intake interview, a caller asked: "So based on what I've told you, is my case worth a lot of money?" The intake specialist, wanting to be helpful, said: "Cases like yours usually settle for around $30K to $50K." The case ultimately settled for $18,000 due to pre-existing conditions.

The client was furious. "Your intake person told me $30K to $50K. Now you're telling me $18K?" The client filed a bar complaint. The firm had to explain why a non-attorney staff member was quoting case values during intake. Intake collects facts. Intake does NOT provide legal advice, quote case values, or promise outcomes. Ever. When a caller asks a legal question, the standard redirect is: "That's a great question — I'll make sure the attorney addresses that with you."

🚫 What Intake Does NOT Do

  • Does NOT provide legal advice of any kind
  • Does NOT promise outcomes, timelines, or settlement amounts
  • Does NOT evaluate case value or comment on liability
  • Does NOT share personal opinions about the case
  • Does NOT use legal jargon — explain in plain language

When asked a legal question: "That's a great question — I'll make sure the attorney addresses that with you."

The Good CasePeer Note Standard: Every communication note must include: (1) WHO you contacted, (2) WHAT was discussed, (3) WHAT WAS DECIDED or learned, (4) NEXT STEPS with tasks set in CasePeer. "Called client" is useless. A detailed note with specifics is the standard.
