Section 1 · Welcome

Case Evaluation Strategy

Every case the firm takes is a bet. Your job is to make sure we only bet on winners.
PA Track — Module 1

Last quarter, the firm accepted a case that should never have passed evaluation.

The client was rear-ended at low speed. Police report listed no injuries. She didn't seek treatment for 47 days. The at-fault driver's carrier had $25K minimum limits. The intake specialist flagged the 47-day gap, but the reviewing attorney overrode it — "she says she's really hurt." Eight months later: $14,000 in medical specials, a defense IME that demolishes causation, and a carrier offering $6,500 to make it go away. The firm has now invested 120+ hours in a case worth less than its cost. Systematic evaluation would have caught this on day one.

📋 What You Will Learn

  • The 10-item Case Safety Checklist — a structured framework for scoring every potential case on liability strength, community danger, and case value
  • Negligence element analysis — duty, breach, causation, damages — and how Georgia's modified comparative fault system (50% bar rule) changes the evaluation calculus
  • Damages valuation — medical specials, general damages drivers, case value benchmarking tiers, and how coverage limits create a ceiling
  • Red flag auto-declines — the eight categories that trigger immediate case rejection unless an attorney specifically overrides with documented reasoning
  • Government entity evaluation — ante litem deadlines, sovereign immunity limitations, and damage caps that fundamentally change whether a case is viable
  • Decision authority — who makes the call at each level and when to escalate to the managing attorney
  • The decline conversation — how to reject a case while preserving the firm's reputation and referral potential
Why Systematic Evaluation? Intuitive case selection feels efficient — experienced attorneys "just know" good cases. But undocumented gut calls produce inconsistent results: accept one weak case and decline a strong one based on the same facts on different days. The evaluation system produces the same answer regardless of which attorney reviews the case or what mood they're in. Consistent case quality is a system output, not a talent output.

Section 2 · The Case Safety Checklist · Up to 40 pts

10 Items That Predict Case Value

A case where the jury cares about the danger is worth 10× a case where they only care about the plaintiff. The Checklist measures community danger — not just individual harm.

⚖️ The 10-Item Case Safety Checklist

Complete this for every potential case. The score drives resource allocation and case prioritization.

1Who else could have been a victim? — Random victim potential. The safety rule applies to everyone, not just this plaintiff. A drunk driver on I-285 endangers every car on the highway. A slip-and-fall on an unmarked wet floor in a grocery store endangers every shopper.
2Is there a clear, simple safety rule the defendant violated? — "A driver must maintain a safe following distance." "A property owner must fix known hazards." If you can't state the rule in one sentence, the jury won't remember it.
3Does the safety rule protect the community, not just this plaintiff? — Community-scope rules activate the jury's self-protective instinct. "This rule protects you, your family, and everyone who uses this road."
4Could it happen again if nothing changes? — If the answer is yes, the jury has a reason to act: their verdict is the enforcement mechanism. If the defendant already fixed the problem, the urgency drops.
5Why should a juror in this community care about this case? — The emotional relevance test. If you can't answer this in 15 seconds, the jury won't care in 15 hours of trial.
6Evidence of defendant's prior knowledge of the danger? — Prior complaints, prior incidents, internal memos, OSHA violations, inspection reports. Knowledge before the incident converts negligence into willful indifference.
7Evidence of prior incidents, complaints, or safety violations? — Pattern evidence. One incident is an accident. Multiple incidents with the same root cause is a choice.
8Can the mechanism of harm be clearly explained? — How the force caused the injury, step by step. In MIST cases especially: a high-school physics teacher can explain how a ton of metal at 6 mph transfers force. The treating physician explains what that force does inside the body.
9Is the plaintiff credible and community-worthy? — "Works, pays taxes, feeds their kids." If the jury doesn't see the plaintiff as worthy, they will invent reasons to give nothing. Once credentialed, jurors who would otherwise ignore the law will follow it.
10Is the injury permanent, life-altering, or significantly impactful on daily function? — Permanency is the single strongest driver of general damages. A herniated disc that resolves in 6 months ≠ a herniated disc that requires surgery and permanently limits mobility.

📊 Scoring Tiers & Resource Allocation

ScoreAssessmentAction
8–10Strong casePrioritize resources — attorney attention, early investigation, expert retention
5–7Average caseProceed normally — standard workflow, monitor for value changes

The Score Is Not the Decision. A case scoring 3/10 on the Safety Checklist is not an automatic decline — but it IS an automatic escalation. Per GLG's evaluation SOP: cases scoring below 5 require review by the Pre-Lit Head before acceptance. The score tells you where to direct attention, not whether to accept.

Section 3 · Liability Assessment · Up to 40 pts

Negligence Elements, Comparative Fault & Causation

A case with devastating injuries and zero liability is worth nothing. Liability is the threshold question — everything else follows.
Real Pattern

The client's story sounds perfect. The liability analysis says otherwise.

Client says: "I was driving straight through the intersection on a green light and the other car ran the red." Police report says: "Both drivers reported green light. No independent witnesses. No traffic camera footage." Without corroborating evidence, this case has a 50/50 disputed liability problem — and under Georgia's 50% bar rule, if the jury assigns even 50% fault to our client, recovery is completely barred.

⚖️ Georgia Negligence Elements — Four Pillars

Every PI case must satisfy ALL FOUR elements. A weakness in any single element is a case-level risk.

ElementWhat You're EvaluatingCommon Failure Mode
DutyDid the defendant owe a legal obligation to protect the plaintiff from harm?Trespasser on private property — minimal duty owed
BreachDid the defendant fail to meet that standard of care?No evidence of what defendant actually did — speculation only
CausationDid the breach directly cause the plaintiff's injuries? (Proximate cause + but-for)Treatment gap destroys causal chain; pre-existing condition clouds causation
DamagesDid the plaintiff suffer actual, compensable harm?No documented treatment; injuries are self-reported only

🚫 Modified Comparative Negligence — The 50% Bar

O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33: Georgia follows a modified comparative negligence system with a hard 50% cutoff.

  • Below 50% fault: Plaintiff recovers, but damages reduced by their percentage of fault. Client at 30% fault on a $100K verdict = $70K recovery.
  • At or above 50% fault: Recovery is completely barred. Zero. 50% is not "reduced by half" — it is total bar.
  • Apportionment to non-parties: Defense can apportion fault to non-parties (§ 51-12-33(c)). The jury considers fault of ALL persons who contributed — including people not named in the lawsuit.
  • No joint liability: Damages are several only. Each defendant pays only their apportioned share. No contribution rights between defendants.
SB 68 Seatbelt Evidence (effective April 21, 2025): For causes of action arising on or after this date, seatbelt non-use is now admissible evidence on negligence, comparative fault, causation, assumption of risk, apportionment, AND to diminish damages. During case evaluation, always determine seatbelt status. A client who was unbuckled at the time of a rear-end collision now faces a comparative fault argument that didn't exist before SB 68. Factor this into every evaluation.
Negligence Per Se: Violation of a statute designed to protect the plaintiff's class = negligence per se. Traffic violations, building code violations, OSHA violations — these establish breach automatically. During evaluation, check: Was the defendant cited? Does the citation map to a statute that protects this class of plaintiff? If yes, breach is established — focus evaluation effort on causation and damages instead.

🔗 Vicarious Liability Theories

  • Respondeat Superior: Employer liable for employee torts committed within scope of employment. Key evaluation question: Was the at-fault driver working? On a delivery? Commuting (generally not within scope)?
  • Negligent Hiring/Retention/Supervision: Direct liability — employer knew or should have known of the employee's unfitness. Prior driving record, prior complaints, lack of training documentation.
  • Owner Liability: Vehicle owner may be liable for permitting an unfit driver to operate their vehicle.

Vicarious liability doubles or triples the available coverage — the at-fault driver's personal policy PLUS the employer's commercial policy. Identifying an employer-defendant can convert a $25K-limits case into a $1M case.

Section 4 · Damages Valuation · Up to 40 pts

Specials, Generals, Coverage Limits & Case Value Drivers

Liability tells you IF the case has value. Damages tell you HOW MUCH. Coverage tells you whether you can collect it.
The Coverage Ceiling

$2.1 million in damages. $25,000 in coverage. What is the case actually worth?

Client suffered a severe spinal cord injury requiring two surgeries. Future medical needs exceed $1.5M. Pain and suffering are devastating. Clear liability — defendant ran a stop sign. But the defendant is uninsured and driving a borrowed car. The owner's policy is minimum limits: $25,000. The client has no UM/UIM coverage. The case "value" is not $2.1 million — it is $25,000. Coverage is the ceiling, not damages.

💰 Special vs. General Damages

Special Damages (Economic)General Damages (Non-Economic)
Medical bills (past + future)Pain and suffering
Lost wages (documented)Loss of enjoyment of life
Future earning capacity reductionEmotional distress
Out-of-pocket expensesLoss of consortium (spouse — 4-year SOL)
Life care plan costsDisfigurement / scarring

SB 68 "Phantom Damages" Change: For causes of action on or after April 21, 2025, both sides can present billed amounts AND amounts actually paid by insurance. Jury sees both numbers. If surgery was billed at $80K but insurer paid $22K, the jury sees both — and decides the "reasonable value." Uninsured plaintiffs may paradoxically recover more for identical treatment.

🔍 Coverage Maximization — The Evaluation Layer Most Attorneys Miss

  • Demand affidavit: When defendant has $250K+ liability limits, demand an affidavit confirming no additional coverage — $250K+ liability often means a $1M umbrella policy exists
  • UM/UIM stacking: Check for add-on vs. traditional UM coverage. Add-on stacks; traditional offsets. Some carriers (State Farm) stack every vehicle as a separate policy
  • Resident relative policies: Every household member's auto policy may provide additional UM coverage
  • Commercial policies: If the defendant was working at the time, the employer's commercial policy applies — this is frequently the difference between a $25K case and a $1M case
  • MedPay: First-party medical payment coverage — no fault required. Collect early to fund treatment
Treatment Gap = Value Killer. A two-week lapse in treatment gives the insurer ammunition to argue the injuries resolved — or were never real. During evaluation, check: When was the accident? When did treatment start? Are there any gaps longer than 14 days? A 47-day gap before first treatment (as in our opening scenario) doesn't just weaken the case — it destroys the causation chain.

Section 5 · Red Flags & Decline Criteria · Up to 40 pts

When to Say No — and How to Say It

Declining the wrong case protects the firm. Declining it well protects the firm's reputation. Both matter equally.
The Decline That Generates Referrals

You declined a case last month. This month, that same person referred two friends with strong cases.

The client's case wasn't viable — single-car accident, no third-party liability. But instead of a cold rejection, the reviewing attorney said: "We want to make sure you get the best representation for your situation." She explained the specific reason, provided a referral to a workers' comp attorney (the client's injuries happened during a work commute), and sent the Declined Case Letter with genuine empathy. The client felt heard, helped, and respected — even though the answer was no. Two weeks later, she sent her brother and her coworker to GLG with viable PI cases.

🚫 Red Flag Auto-Declines — Decline Without Attorney Override

The following are automatic declines unless an attorney specifically overrides with documented reasoning in CasePeer:

#Red FlagWhy It's an Auto-Decline
1Statute of limitations expiredAbsolute bar. No override possible. Taking an expired SOL case = malpractice exposure.
2No third-party liabilitySingle-car accident, self-inflicted. No defendant to recover from. PI requires a negligent third party.
3No medical treatment within 30 days47-day treatment gap = destroyed causation. If the client didn't seek treatment for a month, the defense will argue no real injury.
4Prior representation by another firmFee-splitting complications, likely ethical issues. Why did the other firm drop or get fired?
5Client is clearly at faultNo viable comparative negligence argument. Under the 50% bar rule, a clearly-at-fault client recovers zero.
6Government entity without ante litem capacityIf the deadline for ante litem notice has passed, the claim is barred regardless of the regular SOL.
7Fraud indicatorsConflicting stories, staged accident evidence, multiple prior PI claims. Accepting a fraudulent case = ethics violation + regulatory exposure.
8Non-PI matterWorkers' comp, family law, criminal defense. Outside firm scope. Refer, don't accept.

💬 The Decline Conversation — Safety + Mutual Purpose

Delivering a case rejection is a high-stakes conversation: the potential client is hurt, hopeful, and about to hear "no." The goal is to decline while preserving the relationship and the firm's reputation.

  1. Establish mutual purpose first: "We want to make sure you get the best representation for your situation."
  2. Start with facts, not conclusions: "Based on what we've reviewed, we've identified [specific issue]." Not: "Your case isn't good enough."
  3. Explain the specific reason: Give one clear, honest reason — not a vague "we're not the right fit." Potential clients deserve to understand why.
  4. Offer an alternative: Refer to an appropriate attorney or legal resource whenever possible. "We don't handle workers' comp, but I can recommend someone who does."
  5. Send the Declined Case Letter: Every decline gets a formal letter logged in CasePeer with the reason documented.

⚖️ Decision Authority Matrix

Decision MakerAuthority Scope
Intake Specialist (Priya)Screens against minimum thresholds and red flags. Can decline cases that hit red flag auto-declines. Cannot accept borderline cases.
Pre-Lit Head AttorneyReviews all borderline cases and any case with Safety Checklist score below 5. Can accept or decline within standard parameters.
Managing AttorneyFinal authority on: policy limits above $500K, government defendants, and any case flagged for decline where the client is pushing back.
Georgia Bar Rule 1.1 (Competence): An attorney must not accept a case she cannot competently handle. If a case type falls outside firm expertise — complex medical malpractice, products liability, maritime law — declining is not just smart business, it is an ethical obligation. Accepting and then providing substandard representation is worse than declining and referring.
Rule 1.16(a) — Mandatory Withdrawal: Georgia Bar rules require declining or withdrawing from representation in certain circumstances — including when the lawyer's condition materially impairs ability to represent the client, or when continued representation would result in violation of the Rules of Professional Conduct.

Section 6 · Government Entity Cases & Decision Authority · Up to 40 pts

Ante Litem, Sovereign Immunity & the Cases That Require the Managing Attorney

Government cases play by different rules. Miss an ante litem deadline and no amount of damages, liability strength, or brilliant lawyering can save the claim.
The Deadline That Cannot Be Extended

Seven months after the accident, the attorney realized the defendant was a county vehicle.

The client was injured when a county-owned garbage truck backed into her car. The intake specialist logged "commercial vehicle" without identifying it as government-owned. The attorney evaluated the case as a standard rear-end with strong liability and accepted it. Seven months later, during discovery, the defense raised sovereign immunity and the expired ante litem deadline. The county ante litem notice requirement is 12 months — but nobody sent one. The claim is barred. Permanently. The case is dead, and the firm faces malpractice exposure.

⏰ Ante Litem Notice Deadlines — Memorize These

Entity TypeDeadlineStatuteKey Requirements
City / Municipal6 monthsO.C.G.A. § 36-33-5Written notice, served on proper official, specific dollar amount
County12 monthsO.C.G.A. § 36-11-1Written notice, specific dollar amount
State12 monthsO.C.G.A. § 50-21-26Written notice, Georgia Tort Claims Act compliance

CRITICAL: Missing the ante litem deadline bars the claim entirely — even if the 2-year personal injury SOL hasn't expired. An accident with a city bus at month 7 cannot be saved. The ante litem deadline supersedes and is shorter than the regular SOL.

🛡️ Sovereign Immunity — What It Changes

  • Limited waiver: Georgia waives sovereign immunity for torts of state officers/employees acting within official duties — but with significant exceptions
  • Discretionary function exception: No liability for discretionary acts, whether or not discretion is abused. A hospital employee's decision about patient admission = discretionary = immune.
  • Damage caps: Statutory caps on individual and aggregate claims per occurrence — your case value may be capped regardless of actual damages
  • Exclusive remedy: The Georgia Tort Claims Act is the exclusive remedy against government entities in state court
  • No punitive damages: Punitive damages are generally not available against government entities

📋 Government Case Evaluation — The Different Calculus

Government cases require a fundamentally different cost-benefit analysis:

  • Damage caps reduce recovery ceiling — even a catastrophic injury case may be capped at a fraction of its true value
  • Discretionary immunity eliminates entire categories of claims — many negligence theories that work against private defendants fail against government
  • Shorter deadlines create malpractice risk — 6-month municipal deadline means evaluation and notice must happen within weeks of intake
  • Discovery is harder — open records requests replace standard discovery for pre-suit investigation
  • Per GLG SOP §5: the Managing Attorney retains final authority on ALL government defendant cases — do NOT sign a fee agreement before the Managing Attorney reviews
Ante Litem Dollar Amount Requirement: The ante litem notice must demand a specific dollar amount that covers the full likely verdict. "An amount exceeding $X" is insufficient. If you send an ante litem demanding $100K and the verdict is $500K, the recovery is capped at the amount in the notice. Get the number right the first time.
HB 1344 Impact on Evaluation: Georgia's Insurance Affordability Claims Integrity Act strengthens fraud detection, modifies claims processing timelines, and enhances regulatory enforcement. For case evaluation: expect insurers to conduct more aggressive fraud screening on incoming claims. Ensure every case accepted has clean documentation — conflicting records, suspicious patterns, or incomplete medical timelines will receive more scrutiny than before HB 1344.
