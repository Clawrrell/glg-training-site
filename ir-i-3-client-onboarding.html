Client Onboarding & Welcome Process

The first 48 hours determine whether a client trusts you for the next 48 weeks.
IR-I Track — Module 3

A woman signs her retainer on a Friday afternoon. She's anxious — her car is totaled, she's in pain, and she's never hired a lawyer before. She goes home and waits.

No text message. No email. No call. The entire weekend passes in silence.

By Monday morning, she's googled three other firms. She calls GLG and says: "I signed papers on Friday and nobody's contacted me. Did you even get my case? I don't think this is going to work out."

The case was strong. The intake was clean. The screening was perfect. But the onboarding — the part where the client starts feeling like they made the right choice — never happened. The firm lost the case before anyone started working on it.

  • Fee agreement explanation — how to explain contingency, percentages, and costs in plain language without crossing the legal advice line

  • Treatment referral & Letter of Protection — connecting clients to providers on lien, explaining the LOP, and knowing when to escalate

Your onboarding role is the welcome text, the welcome packet email, document follow-up, and preparing the client for the CM's call. You need to understand the full welcome flow so you can set accurate expectations, but you don't execute the CM's script.

Section 3 · Fee Agreement Explanation · Up to 40 pts

Explaining the Fee Agreement Without Crossing the Line

What the client signs, in plain language. What it means legally — that's the attorney's job.
The Moment That Builds or Breaks Trust

A client stares at the retainer agreement and says: "I don't understand any of this. What does contingency mean? Am I signing away a third of everything?"

Their anxiety is rising. They're thinking about money they don't have. They're wondering if they're being taken advantage of. What you say next either builds trust or destroys it.

You have exactly the right answer — clear, accurate, reassuring. But you also know the line you cannot cross. You explain what the agreement says. You do NOT advise them on whether it's a good deal, whether they should negotiate, or what it means for their specific situation. That line is the difference between helping and practicing law without a license.

💰 Safe Language for Fee Explanation

  • "Contingency" = "We only get paid if we recover money for you. If we don't win, you owe us nothing for our time."
  • "33.3%" = "The firm's fee is 33.3% of your recovery if we resolve the case before filing a lawsuit."
  • "40%" = "If the case goes to litigation — meaning we have to file a lawsuit — the fee is 40%."
  • "Costs vs. Fees" = "Costs are separate from our attorney fee. Things like medical records, filing fees, and expert reports are advanced by the firm during the case and reimbursed from the settlement. You don't pay them out of pocket."
🔴 UPL RED LINES — Never Say These:
❌ "33% is a good deal / competitive / fair"
❌ "You should / shouldn't sign this"
❌ "I can try to get the fee lowered for you"
❌ "Based on your case, I think..."
✅ Instead: "Let me have the attorney speak with you about that — I want to make sure you get the most thorough answer."

Section 4 · HIPAA & Medical Authorization · Up to 40 pts

Explaining the Medical Release — Reassurance, Not Legal Advice

The client needs to understand what they're signing. They don't need a lecture on federal privacy law.
The Trust Test

The client freezes at the HIPAA form. "I have medical things I don't want anyone knowing about."

This is the moment where clients either trust you or shut down. They're not objecting to the form — they're expressing fear. Fear that their private information will be exposed. Fear that signing means losing control.

Your job is to address the emotion first, then the information. Acknowledge the concern before explaining the scope. A client who feels heard will sign. A client who feels dismissed will walk.

📋 What the Authorization Does

  • Allows GLG to request medical records from providers who treated the client's accident injuries
  • Records are used to build the case — documenting the nature and extent of injuries
  • Covers records related to the accident injuries only — not the client's entire medical history

🚫 What the Authorization Does NOT Do

  • Does NOT give GLG access to unrelated medical history
  • Does NOT authorize sharing records with third parties beyond what's needed for the case
  • Does NOT waive the client's medical privacy rights broadly
✅ Safe Language: "This authorization allows us to request your medical records from your doctors so we can build your case. It only covers records related to your accident injuries — nothing else. Your attorney can walk you through the specifics if you'd like."
🔴 Never Say: "This gives us full access to your medical history" / "You have to sign this or we can't help you" / "I can explain your HIPAA rights" — you collect the form, you don't interpret privacy law.

Section 5 · Treatment Referral & Letter of Protection · Up to 40 pts

Connecting Clients to Treatment — The Foundation of Every Case

No treatment = no records = no case. This is where you start building the foundation.
The Client Who Can't Afford a Doctor

A client says: "I can't afford to go to the doctor right now." They're in pain. They think medical care requires money they don't have.

If nobody explains the Letter of Protection, this client stops treating. Without treatment records, the case loses its foundation. The insurance company points to the gap and says: "If they were really hurt, they would have gone to the doctor."

One sentence changes everything: "You pay nothing upfront. We work with providers who get paid from your settlement, not from your pocket." That sentence keeps the client in treatment and the case alive.

📋 LOP in Plain Language

  • What it is: A Letter of Protection (LOP) is a guarantee from the firm that a medical provider's bill will be paid from the client's settlement when the case closes
  • What to tell the client: "We work with medical providers who treat on a lien basis — meaning they get paid from your settlement when the case closes, not from your pocket right now. You pay nothing upfront."
  • Client asks "What if we lose?": ESCALATE TO ATTORNEY. Do NOT improvise on LOP risk allocation. This is a legal question about contractual liability.

🏥 Initiating the Treatment Referral

  1. Ask: "Do you prefer a provider closer to your home or your work?"
  2. Note the client's current treatment status (treating, not yet treating, or stopped)
  3. Create the referral in CasePeer
  4. Explain the LOP concept in plain language
Why Treatment Compliance Matters: Medical records are the foundation of the case. Complete, consistent treatment records give the firm the most leverage during negotiations. When a client misses appointments or stops treating early, the insurance company uses those gaps to argue the injuries weren't serious — and that directly reduces the settlement value.

Section 6 · Onboarding Communication & Handoff · Up to 40 pts

Setting Expectations & Preparing the Client for Their Case Manager

You built the trust. Now hand it off without dropping it.
The Handoff That Breaks Trust

You've done everything right — welcome text sent within the hour, fee explained clearly, HIPAA concern resolved, treatment referral initiated. The client feels great.

Then five days of silence. No CM call. No follow-up. No one reaches out. The trust you built in 2 hours evaporates in 5 days of nothing.

The handoff is the bridge between your onboarding work and the CM's ongoing relationship. If the client doesn't know who calls next, when, and why — they assume nobody is coming.

🤝 The Handoff Script

Tell the client exactly what happens next:

"Your case manager [CM Name] will call you within 24 hours to introduce themselves and walk you through your case timeline and answer any detailed questions. They'll be your main point of contact for the rest of your case. In the meantime, I'm here if you need anything."

🔍 Coverage Maximization — What to Flag

Detailed coverage analysis is CM/attorney work. But during onboarding, flag these for the Coverage Maximization Checklist:

  • UM/UIM coverage status — Does the client have uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage?
  • MedPay availability — Does the client's auto insurance include medical payments coverage?
  • At-fault driver's insurance status — Is the other driver insured? What carrier?
