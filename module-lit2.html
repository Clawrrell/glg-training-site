Section 2 · Types of Discovery & Georgia Rules · Up to 40 pts

The Five Discovery Devices

O.C.G.A. §§ 9-11-26 through 9-11-37 — the rules that govern how evidence is exchanged.

⚖️ Georgia's Five Discovery Devices

DeviceStatuteKey Rule
Interrogatories§ 9-11-33Written questions to parties. Max 50 (including subparts). 30-day response deadline. Failure to respond waives objections.
Requests for Production§ 9-11-34Documents, ESI, tangible things. 30-day response deadline. Must produce as kept in ordinary course or labeled to match request categories.
Requests for Admission§ 9-11-36MOST DANGEROUS DEVICE. 30-day deadline. No response = deemed admitted as established fact. Can be case-ending.
Depositions§ 9-11-30/31Oral or written examination under oath. Reasonable notice required. Only valid ground to instruct not to answer: privilege.
Subpoenas§ 9-11-45Third-party document requests and testimony. Used for non-parties (medical providers, employers, witnesses).

🔍 Discovery Scope — O.C.G.A. § 9-11-26(b)

Broad scope: Parties may obtain discovery regarding any matter, not privileged, which is relevant to the subject matter involved in the pending action. Information need not be admissible at trial if it appears reasonably calculated to lead to the discovery of admissible evidence.

Key limitations: Attorney-client privilege, work product doctrine, and protective orders (§ 9-11-26(c)) can restrict discovery.

The RFA trap: Requests for Admission are the only discovery device where silence = automatic loss. If you miss a 30-day RFA deadline, every requested fact is deemed admitted. The defense can then move for summary judgment based on your client's "admissions." This has destroyed cases at GLG-peer firms. Calendar RFA deadlines as the highest-priority discovery deadline.

Section 3 · Drafting & Responding · Up to 40 pts

Drafting & Responding to Discovery Requests

What you ask, how you respond, and the duty to supplement — every word matters.

📝 Drafting Discovery Requests — GLG Best Practices

  • Interrogatories: Start with the most critical information. In trucking cases: driver qualifications, training, hours-of-service compliance, driving record. In standard PI: liability facts, witness identification, insurance coverage.
  • Requests for Production: Cast a targeted net — ask for categories of documents, not individual documents. Example: "All documents relating to maintenance of the vehicle involved in the collision."
  • Requests for Admission: Use strategically to narrow issues for trial. Admit uncontested facts early (date of accident, ownership of vehicle) to focus trial on real disputes.
  • Georgia limit: 50 interrogatories maximum including subparts (O.C.G.A. § 9-11-33(a)). Plan carefully — you cannot exceed this without court permission.

🛡 Responding to Discovery — The Objection Framework

ObjectionWhen to UseKey Rule
Overly BroadRequest seeks information far beyond the scope of the case"Describe all medical treatment for any condition in the past 10 years" in a recent auto accident case
Unduly BurdensomeCost/effort of compliance disproportionate to the value of the informationMust explain the specific burden — blanket claims are overruled
PrivilegeRequest seeks attorney-client or work product materialsMust provide a privilege log identifying each withheld document
Vague / AmbiguousRequest is unclear about what is actually being soughtState the objection, then answer to the extent you understand the request

Golden rule: Object specifically, then respond to the extent the request is not objectionable. Blanket refusals without any response are disfavored and often overruled.

🔄 Duty to Supplement — O.C.G.A. § 9-11-26(e)

The duty to supplement is ongoing and affirmative. When you learn that a prior response is materially incomplete or incorrect — such as a new surgery, additional treatment, or newly discovered documents — you must supplement "seasonably" (without unreasonable delay). Failure to supplement can result in exclusion of evidence at trial.

HIPAA does not block discovery. HIPAA governs healthcare providers' disclosure of medical information — it does not apply between litigants in a lawsuit. Citing HIPAA as an objection to discovery is improper and will be overruled. Use specific, recognized objections (overbreadth, privilege) instead.

Discovery Calendar Management

A missed deadline is not a mistake — it is a preventable failure of systems.

📅 Deadline Management System

EventDeadline RuleCalendar Action
Interrogatories Received30 days from date of serviceCalendar response deadline + reminders at 14, 7, and 3 days
RFPs Received30 days from date of serviceCalendar response deadline + begin document review immediately
RFAs Received30 days — AUTOMATIC ADMISSION IF MISSEDCalendar as HIGHEST PRIORITY — flag attorney immediately
Deposition NoticedReasonable notice (typically 30 days)Calendar depo date, prep session 3-5 days before, document review 1 week before
Discovery CutoffSet by court scheduling orderWork BACKWARD — all discovery must be initiated in time to be completed before cutoff

⏰ Computing Time — O.C.G.A. § 9-11-6(a)

  • Day 1 is the day AFTER the event — do not count the day of service
  • Weekend/holiday rule: If the deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, it extends to the next business day
  • Three-day mail rule: When service is by mail, add 3 days to the response deadline
  • Working backward: From a discovery cutoff, count backward to determine the latest date you can initiate each discovery device and still receive responses before the cutoff

📊 CasePeer Status Transitions

Service → Discovery

When the defendant files their answer, the case transitions from "Service" to "Discovery" in CasePeer. This status change triggers the discovery workflow: create the discovery calendar, initiate GLG's first set of discovery requests, and begin tracking all deadlines. A case stuck in "Service" status after the answer is filed = a process failure visible on the scorecard.

The backward planning rule: If the discovery cutoff is October 15 and you need to depose three witnesses, you cannot notice depositions on October 1. Expert depositions require 4-6 weeks of lead time for scheduling, notice, and potential rescheduling. Always work backward from the cutoff to determine the latest safe date to initiate each discovery action.

Section 5 · Production & Privilege · Up to 40 pts

Document Production, Privilege & Bates Numbering

Every page produced creates a record. Every page withheld must be justified.

🔒 Privilege Review Process

  1. Collect all responsive documents — everything that falls within the scope of the request
  2. Review each document for privilege — attorney-client communications, work product (attorney mental impressions, strategies, legal theories)
  3. Withhold privileged documents — do NOT produce them
  4. Create a privilege log entry for each withheld document: date, author, recipient(s), general subject matter, specific privilege asserted
  5. Produce all non-privileged responsive documents
  6. Flag any uncertain items for attorney review before production

📋 Privilege Log Requirements

A proper privilege log entry must contain enough information for opposing counsel to assess the privilege claim without revealing the privileged content:

ElementExample
DateMarch 15, 2026
Author/SenderJohn Martinez (client)
Recipient(s)H. Gunn (attorney)
Subject MatterCase strategy discussion
Privilege AssertedAttorney-Client Privilege

Vague entries like "privileged — do not produce" are insufficient and may result in waiver of the privilege.

🏷 Bates Numbering

Purpose: Create a unique, sequential identifier for every page produced so any page can be referenced precisely during depositions, motions, and trial.

Format: [CASE-PREFIX]-[SEQUENTIAL NUMBER] — Example: MARTINEZ-0001, MARTINEZ-0002, etc.

Production organization (O.C.G.A. § 9-11-34(b)): Documents must be produced either (1) as they are kept in the ordinary course of business, or (2) organized and labeled to correspond with the categories in the request.

Spoliation = destruction of evidence. If you discover a document during review that is harmful to your client, you CANNOT delete, destroy, or hide it. That is spoliation — a sanctionable offense that can result in adverse inference instructions, monetary sanctions, or case dismissal. Harmful documents are withheld only if privileged. Otherwise, they must be produced.

Discovery Disputes, E-Discovery & Your Role

When discovery breaks down — and what litigation support staff must do every day.

⚖️ Discovery Dispute Resolution — The Enforcement Ladder

StepActionKey Rule
1 — Meet & ConferGood-faith letter or phone call attempting to resolve without court interventionRequired before most courts will hear a discovery motion. Document all attempts.
2 — Motion to CompelFile motion with certificate of good-faith effort. Court orders compliance within specified timeframe.O.C.G.A. § 9-11-37(a). Court may award attorney fees to the prevailing party.
3 — SanctionsIf a party violates a court order to compel, the court may impose sanctions.O.C.G.A. § 9-11-37(b): monetary sanctions, facts deemed established, pleadings stricken, default judgment.

💻 E-Discovery Basics for PI Cases

  • Spoliation letters: Sent immediately after engagement (especially trucking cases) demanding preservation of ECM data, dashcam footage, driver logs, maintenance records, and all ESI. Per the Injury Case Playbook: trucking companies deploy response teams within 2 hours — spoliation letters must go out the same day.
  • ESI (Electronically Stored Information): Text messages, emails, social media posts, GPS data, black box data, phone records. These are discoverable under O.C.G.A. § 9-11-34.
  • Preservation obligation: Once litigation is reasonably anticipated, both parties have a duty to preserve relevant evidence — including ESI. A spoliation letter documents this obligation.
  • Social media: Client's and defendant's social media posts are discoverable. At intake, GLG sends the CYA letter warning clients about social media activity during litigation.

👩‍💼 Your Role — Litigation Support Staff

Your PRIMARY responsibilities during discovery:

  • 📅 Calendar every deadline within 24 hours of receipt — interrogatories, RFPs, RFAs, depositions, discovery cutoff
  • 📁 Organize document productions — Bates numbering, categorization, index preparation
  • 📋 Maintain the privilege log — track every withheld document with proper entries
  • 🔔 Flag issues for attorney review — uncertain privilege calls, tight deadlines, opposing counsel non-compliance
  • 📊 Track discovery status — what has been sent, what is outstanding, what is overdue
  • 📞 Follow up — when opposing counsel is late, confirm receipt of productions, chase down outstanding responses

What you do NOT do: Decide which objections to assert, negotiate discovery disputes with opposing counsel, or independently draft/sign discovery responses. These are attorney functions.

The staff multiplier effect: An attorney with excellent litigation support staff can manage 3x the caseload without missing deadlines. Your organizational work is what makes the attorney's legal work possible. Per the Essential Lawyering memo: "the ratio of preparation to performance can easily reach 15 or 20 to 1."
