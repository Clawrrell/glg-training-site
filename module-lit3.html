Section 1 · Welcome

Service of Process

A filed case that is never served is the same as a case that was never filed. Failed service = dismissed cases, malpractice exposure, and harm to clients.
LIT Track — Module 3

The complaint was filed 8 months ago. The defendant has never been served. The statute of limitations ran 6 months ago.

A paralegal filed the complaint and sent the summons to the process server. The server made one attempt, didn't find the defendant home, and the file sat. No follow-up. No escalation. No CasePeer documentation of any service activity for 7 months. When the defendant's attorney finally appeared, they moved to dismiss — arguing the plaintiff failed to exercise any diligence in perfecting service. The court agreed. Case dismissed. The client's case — and their right to recover — died because of a procedural failure that was entirely preventable.

Section 2 · The 5-Day Window & Standard Process · Up to 40 pts

Relation-Back and Same-Day Initiation

The 5-day window is the golden standard. Miss it and you're fighting uphill on diligence for the rest of the case.

⏱️ The 5-Day Relation-Back Window — O.C.G.A. § 9-11-4(c)

If the process server receives the summons and complaint and serves the defendant within 5 days, service relates back to the filing date as a matter of law. No questions asked. No diligence analysis required.

  • The clock starts when the person making service (sheriff or process server) receives the summons and complaint — not when the complaint is filed (Giles v. State Farm Mut. Ins. Co., 330 Ga. App. 314 (2014) (en banc))
  • In practice: When the clerk delivers the stamped summons same-day, the filing date and the clock-start date coincide
  • Critical nuance: Filing proof of service (the return) is separate from performing service. Proof must be filed within 5 business days of service per O.C.G.A. § 9-11-4(h), but failure to file proof does not invalidate the service itself

📋 Same-Day Initiation — The Standard Process

StepActionOwnerDeadline
1File complaint with courtLit ParalegalPer SOL/case timeline
2Confirm stamped complaint returned by courtLit ParalegalSame day as filing
3Send summons and complaint to process serverLit ParalegalSame day complaint returned
4Log service initiation in CasePeerLit ParalegalSame day

The goal: Get the summons to the process server the same day the clerk returns the stamped complaint. This maximizes the chance of service within the 5-day relation-back window.

📝 CasePeer Documentation at Initiation

The following must be logged in CasePeer the same day service is initiated:

  • Date complaint filed
  • Date summons issued by clerk
  • Date summons delivered to process server (start of 5-day clock)
  • Process server name/company and tracking number
  • Defendant address(es) for service
Federal Court Removal: If a case filed in Georgia state court is removed to federal court, Georgia's diligence standard — not Federal Rule 4(m)'s 90-day window — governs whether service relates back. Georgia courts have interpreted their service statute as "integral parts of the state statute of limitations," making it substantive law under Erie. (Wilson v. Hearos, LLC, 128 F.4th 1254 (11th Cir. 2025))

Section 3 · The Two-Tier Diligence Standard · Up to 30 pts

Reasonable Diligence vs. Greatest Possible Diligence

When service misses the 5-day window and the SOL has run, courts will scrutinize your efforts. The standard depends on what the defendant does — not what you know.

⚖️ The Two Tiers — Settled Law

TierStandardWhen It AppliesKey Authority
Tier 1Reasonable Diligence — plaintiff must show "reasonable and diligent" efforts to perfect service as quickly as possibleDefault when SOL has expired but defendant has not yet raised a service defense in courtRobinson v. Boyd, 288 Ga. 53 (2010); Van Omen v. Lopresti, 357 Ga. App. 9 (2020)
Tier 2Greatest Possible Diligence — plaintiff must demonstrate "the greatest possible diligence from that point forward"Triggered only when defendant raises the issue of defective service in a court filing (answer or motion)Van Omen v. Lopresti, 357 Ga. App. 9 (2020); Griffin v. Trinidad, 357 Ga. App. 492 (2020)
CRITICAL LEGAL DISTINCTION: The trigger for Tier 2 is the defendant's court filing raising the service defense — NOT the plaintiff's "mere awareness" that service is a problem. Van Omen v. Lopresti (2020) resolved a conflict in prior case law and overruled the competing "awareness" line of cases. If someone tells you awareness alone triggers Tier 2, they are citing overruled law.

📋 What "Diligence" Requires in Practice

  • Continuous, proactive efforts — not passive waiting for the sheriff to get around to it
  • All available legal channels pursued — you cannot rely on a single method and stop
  • No unexplained gaps — every period between attempts must be accounted for
  • Specific dates and details — not conclusory statements like "I tried everything"
  • The showing must demonstrate the plaintiff attempted to ensure service was made "as quickly as possible"

💀 What Kills a Case on Diligence

  • Filing and doing nothing for months — Long v. Bellamy, 296 Ga. App. 263 (2009): over a year without service after renewal filing = dismissed
  • Relying solely on one method without following up or escalating — Neely v. Jones, 271 Ga. App. 487 (2005): failure to pursue alternative methods after initial failures = dismissed
  • Shifting the burden onto the court — Kelley v. Lymon, 279 Ga. App. 849 (2006): the plaintiff cannot expect the court to locate the defendant

Section 4 · The Escalation Ladder · Up to 40 pts

Steps 1–6: From Substituted Service to Publication

When initial personal service fails, you don't stop — you escalate. Each step has a trigger, a method, and documentation requirements.
Why This Matters

The process server says "unable to serve." That is not where the process ends — it is where the escalation begins.

An "unable to serve" return from the process server is Step 0. The escalation ladder defines every step after that. Skipping steps, going out of order, or failing to document each step creates gaps that a defendant's attorney will exploit. Every rung of the ladder matters.

🪜 The Six-Step Escalation Ladder

StepMethodTriggerAuthority
1Substituted Service — leave summons with a person of suitable age/discretion who actually resides at the defendant's dwellingInitial personal service fails (defendant not present)O.C.G.A. § 9-11-4(e)(7); Bible v. Bible, 259 Ga. 418 (1989)
2Waiver of Service by Mail — mail request asking defendant to voluntarily waive formal serviceSubstituted service impractical (no one at residence on multiple attempts)O.C.G.A. § 9-11-4(f)
3Motion for Special Appointment — court appoints specialized private process serverStandard server/sheriff cannot locate or reach defendantCourt order required
4Secretary of State Service — serve GA SOS as registered agent (entity defendants only)Registered agent cannot be found/served at registered addressO.C.G.A. § 14-2-504 (corps); § 14-11-209 (LLCs)
5Motion for Alternative Service — NOT AVAILABLE IN GEORGIA for original process on a resident defendant. Email, social media, and door-posting are not authorized methods. See the correction notice below.—No authorizing provision; § 9-11-4(j) is conditional only
6Service by Publication — publish notice in county newspaper for 4 consecutive weeks LAST RESORTAll other methods failed, defendant truly unlocatableO.C.G.A. § 9-11-4(f)(1)(A)
⚠️ CORRECTION NOTICE — July 27, 2026. An earlier version of this module stated that publication provides only in rem jurisdiction, and listed a general "motion for alternative service" as Step 5. Both statements were wrong. The corrected law is below. If you trained on the prior version, re-read this section.
⚠️ Publication — The General Rule: Service by publication does not confer personal jurisdiction in a Georgia tort action, and without personal jurisdiction no money judgment may be recovered. Henderson v. James, 350 Ga. App. 361, 364 (2019); Southeastern Sec. Ins. Co. v. Lowe, 242 Ga. App. 535, 536(1) (2000) ("There is no provision in this state whereby courts may acquire jurisdiction over a defendant by service by publication and then render an in personam judgment against him."). Attorney AND Managing Attorney approval required before filing any publication motion.

⚖️ THE MELTON EXCEPTION — When Publication CAN Support a Money Judgment

Do not treat publication as a dead end without running this analysis first. Melton v. Johnson, 242 Ga. 400 (1978) — Georgia Supreme Court, still good law, expressly reaffirmed in Henderson v. James (2019) — holds that publication service can support an in personam money judgment where the defendant:

#ElementWhat proves it
1Is a Georgia resident actually present within the court's jurisdictionSkip trace, property/utility/voter records, vehicle registration confirming current in-state residence
2Has ACTUAL KNOWLEDGE that a suit is pending against him⚠️ THE HARD ONE — see below
3Willfully conceals himself to frustrate all reasonable efforts at personal serviceServer affidavits, evasion evidence, occupant statements, repeated failed attempts at varied times

The statutory hook is O.C.G.A. § 9-11-4(f)(1)(A), which lists "conceals himself or herself to avoid the service of the summons" as an independent ground and provides that the Code section "shall apply to all manner of civil actions."

⚠️ Element 2 is where cases die. Evading a process server is NOT the same as knowing a lawsuit exists. An occupant saying "nobody by that name lives here" proves evasion, not knowledge. Element 2 must be built deliberately and early — not reconstructed after the fact. Build it with: certified mail return receipt requested to every known address (a refused or unclaimed receipt is affirmative evidence the defendant knew); an ignored waiver-of-service request; documented phone contact referencing the suit; correspondence from the defendant's insurer or counsel acknowledging the suit (often the strongest single item); server affidavits reciting occupant statements showing they knew why the server was there. If Element 2 cannot be documented, the Melton exception is unavailable and the general rule controls.

❌ What Is NOT Available in Georgia — Corrected July 27, 2026

Georgia is a strict-compliance state. Courts cannot invent service methods, and actual notice does not cure defective service.

Bible v. Bible, 259 Ga. 418 (1989): "We hold OCGA § 9-11-4 (d) (7) means exactly what it states, and that service under this section must be made as provided." Bible overruled the prior "substantial compliance" rule. (Former § 9-11-4(d)(7) is the same provision now lettered § 9-11-4(e)(7) — re-lettering only; the holding stands.) It is "immaterial whether the defendant had actual notice." IMC Constr. v. Mitchell, 365 Ga. App. 470 (2022).

There is no general "such other means as the court may direct" provision in Georgia. That language is FRCP 4(f)(3) — a federal rule. Do not cite it in a Georgia court. Georgia's only catch-all is § 9-11-4(j), which activates only where service is "not prescribed by law" or "not clear or certain." For a Georgia-resident defendant the method is prescribed — § 9-11-4(e)(7) — so (j) cannot authorize email, social media, or posting.

MethodAvailable for original process on a GA resident?Authority

Publication⚠️ For notice yes; for a money judgment only under Melton§ 9-11-4(f)(1)(A)

Service by social media❌ No — no Georgia appellate authority approving it—

������ The mailing question — corrected. § 9-11-4(e)(7) does NOT require a follow-up mailing to the defendant after leaving papers with a co-resident. The mail step exists only in § 9-11-4(e)(6) (the under-$200 tack-and-mail provision) and in the Federal Rules. If you were taught that (e)(7) requires a mailing, that was wrong. Mail it anyway — it is harmless, and it builds the Melton actual-knowledge record — but never conclude service was defective merely because no mailing went out.

🔍 Pre-Publication Diligence — The Affidavit of Diligent Search

Before any court will grant a motion for publication, the plaintiff must compile an Affidavit of Diligent Search documenting all of these steps. The constitutional prerequisite is "a showing that reasonable diligence has been exercised in attempting to ascertain their whereabouts," and it is "the duty of the courts to determine whether the movant has exercised due diligence in pursuing every reasonably available channel of information." Abba Gana v. Abba Gana, 251 Ga. 340 (1983). The showing must be made by affidavit with specific facts — dates, addresses, methods, outcomes. Conclusory statements fail.

  1. USPS FOIA address search
  2. Professional skip trace (LexisNexis, TLO, or IRB Search)
  3. Tax assessor records in defendant's and surrounding counties
  4. Voter registration search
  5. Court filing index search (superior, state, magistrate)
  6. DMV records for current license address
  7. Employer/workplace service attempts
  8. Social media / online presence check

Section 5 · Trucking Case Service Chains · Up to 40 pts

Parallel Tracks: Individual, Entity, and Nonresident Defendants

Trucking cases have multiple defendant types. Every track launches simultaneously on Day 3. Never run serial loops — always parallel.
Trucking Case Architecture

A trucking case typically has 3+ defendants: the driver, the trucking company, and possibly the company owner. Each requires a different service track — and all run at the same time.

The core principle: service tracks run in PARALLEL by defendant type from Day 1. Never run serial skip-trace → attempt → skip-trace → attempt loops. Every defendant gets simultaneous, type-appropriate service tracks the moment the complaint is filed.

🚛 The Three Service Tracks

TrackDefendant TypeService MethodKey Authority
Track AIndividual (drivers, owners)Personal service at residence (3 attempts at varied times) → workplace → secondary addressesO.C.G.A. § 9-11-4(e)(7)
Track BEntity (corps, LLCs, carriers)Serve registered agent + BOC-3 agent + certified mail to principal office — all simultaneouslyO.C.G.A. § 14-2-504 / § 14-11-209; 49 CFR § 366
Track CNonresident (out-of-state driver)Nonresident Motorist Act: serve GA SOS + certified mail to out-of-state address + file affidavit of complianceO.C.G.A. § 40-12-1 et seq.

📡 FMCSA SAFER System — Required for Motor Carriers

For every carrier defendant, look up the carrier at safer.fmcsa.dot.gov on Day 1 and record:

  • MC number and DOT number
  • Operating status
  • BOC-3 process agent designated for Georgia (49 CFR § 366) — this is often your fastest entity service path
  • Insurance carrier from SAFER filing

🏛️ Nonresident Motorist Act — O.C.G.A. § 40-12-1

For out-of-state driver defendants, the Nonresident Motorist Act provides a statutory service method. Three requirements must ALL be satisfied:

  1. ✅ Secretary of State served with summons and complaint
  2. ✅ Certified mail (return receipt requested) sent to defendant at out-of-state address
  3. ✅ Affidavit of compliance filed with the court

The SOS must certify to the court within 3 days of receiving service. The defendant has 30 days from SOS certification to file an answer.

Registered Agent = Owner: If the entity's registered agent is also an individual defendant (common in small trucking companies), service attempts at that address count for both Track A and Track B. Flag this immediately — if personal service on the owner/RA fails, it accelerates SOS service for the entity.

Section 6 · Post-SOL Protocol, Renewal & Documentation · Up to 40 pts

When the Clock Has Run and the Defendant Isn't Served

Post-SOL service is a crisis. The renewal action is a strategic reset. Documentation standards are the foundation for both.
Emergency Protocol

The SOL expired 3 weeks ago. The defendant hasn't been served. The managing attorney just found out.

This is not a normal service follow-up. This is a crisis. Every day of inaction from this point forward is a day the defendant's attorney will cite in a dismissal motion. The team has 24 hours to audit the service file, identify every gap, and launch every untried method simultaneously.

🚨 Post-SOL Emergency Protocol

  1. IMMEDIATE: Alert case attorney AND managing attorney
  2. Within 24 hours: Audit the service file — list every attempt with date/time, identify any gaps > 14 days, identify any methods NOT yet tried
  3. Within 48 hours: Launch ALL untried methods simultaneously — second skip trace (different database), SOS service if not yet filed, PI surveillance if not yet retained, substituted service if a qualifying co-resident can be identified, and certified mail return receipt requested to every known address to begin building the Melton actual-knowledge record
  4. Ongoing: Document EVERYTHING — every call, every search, every attempt. 14-day maximum between documented actions. No action = affirmative decision documented with reasoning
  5. Begin compiling Affidavit of Diligent Search immediately — must be ready to file the moment defendant challenges service

🔄 Renewal Action Strategy — O.C.G.A. § 9-2-61(a)

ATTORNEY-DECISION ONLY — not paralegal discretion.

If service is proving extremely difficult, a voluntary dismissal followed by a renewal action resets the diligence clock. The Georgia Supreme Court held that "diligence in perfecting service of process in an action properly refiled under O.C.G.A. § 9-2-61(a) must be measured from the time of filing the renewed suit" — any delay in service in the original action is not available as a defense in the renewal. (Robinson v. Boyd, 288 Ga. 53, 701 S.E.2d 165 (2010))

Requirements:

  • The original action must have been filed within the statute of limitations
  • The plaintiff must voluntarily dismiss BEFORE the trial court rules the case should be dismissed for lack of diligence
  • The renewal must be filed within 6 months of the dismissal
  • This renewal privilege may be exercised only ONCE if the dismissal occurs after the SOL has expired

📝 Documentation Standards — The 14-Day Rule

No case should go silent for more than 14 days without a documented service action or a documented reason for the gap.

StatusMeaning
SERVICE INITIATEDSummons sent to process server
SERVICE PENDINGAttempts in progress
SERVICE FAILED — ESCALATINGMoving to next ladder step
SERVICE COMPLETEDSuccessful — proof of service filed
SERVICE — PUBLICATION REVIEWAll methods exhausted, evaluating publication
Renewal ≠ Immunity. While the renewal resets the diligence clock, the team must still demonstrate diligence from the renewal filing date forward. Long v. Bellamy (296 Ga. App. 263) dismissed a case where the plaintiff refiled under § 9-2-61(a) but then did nothing for over a year. The reset only helps if the team actually uses it.
