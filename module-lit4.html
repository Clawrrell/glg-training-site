Four Deposition Types — Four Different Prep Lists

Every deposition type has a different staff checklist. Know which one you are prepping.

📋 The Four Deposition Types in PI Litigation

TypeWho Is DeposedStaff Responsibilities
Plaintiff DepoOur client — deposed by defense counselSchedule, coordinate with client, prepare exhibit copies, confirm location/video, brief client on logistics (NOT testimony — attorney does that)
Defendant DepoThe at-fault party — deposed by our attorneyNotice the deposition, coordinate court reporter, prepare attorney's exhibit set, organize prior statements/police report for attorney review
Expert/IME DepoDefense medical examiner or our expertConfirm expert availability, coordinate scheduling with all parties, prepare medical records and prior reports, arrange video if needed
Treating PhysicianClient's doctor — deposed by either sideCoordinate with physician's office (availability, location), prepare complete treatment records, ensure HIPAA authorization is current, confirm records match what doctor received

⚖️ Staff vs. Attorney — The Bright Line

Staff handles: Scheduling, logistics, exhibits, court reporter booking, location coordination, client travel arrangements, document compilation, CasePeer updates.

Attorney handles: Testimony preparation with client, deposition strategy, question outlines, objection decisions, substantive witness prep. Staff must never coach a witness on what to say.

Ethics line: Staff preparing a client for deposition logistics (what to wear, where to go, what to bring) = appropriate. Staff telling a client how to answer questions = unauthorized practice of law. The distinction is absolute.

Section 3 · Scheduling & Notice Requirements · Up to 40 pts

Georgia Deposition Notice Rules

Proper notice is jurisdictional. Get it wrong and the deposition is void — or worse, sanctionable.

⚖️ Georgia Deposition Notice Requirements

RuleRequirementCitation
Reasonable NoticeGeorgia requires "reasonable notice in writing" to every party. Courts generally consider 10 business days the minimum for reasonableness.O.C.G.A. § 9-11-30(b)(1)
ContentMust state: deponent name, date, time, and location. If documents requested, must attach or describe them.O.C.G.A. § 9-11-30(b)(1)
Non-PartiesNon-party witnesses (including treating physicians) require a subpoena in addition to the notice. A notice alone has no compulsory power over non-parties.O.C.G.A. § 9-11-45
Written QuestionsDepositions on written questions require the questions to be served on all parties, who have 30 days to serve cross-questions.O.C.G.A. § 9-11-31
LocationPlaintiff may be required to attend in the county where the action is pending. Non-party witnesses: within 150 miles of their residence or place of business.O.C.G.A. § 24-13-24

📋 Staff Scheduling Checklist — Every Deposition

  1. Confirm deponent availability (coordinate with their counsel or office if non-party)
  2. Book court reporter — confirm they provide real-time or rough draft if attorney needs it
  3. Reserve conference room or virtual platform (Zoom for remote depositions)
  4. If videographed: book videographer separately from court reporter
  5. Prepare and serve Notice of Deposition on all parties (10+ business days)
  6. If non-party: serve subpoena under O.C.G.A. § 9-11-45 (requires witness fee + mileage)
  7. Calendar the deposition date in CasePeer with reminders at 7 days and 1 day
  8. Compile exhibit set for attorney review at least 3 business days before
Witness fees: Under O.C.G.A. § 24-13-27, a subpoenaed witness is entitled to a witness fee ($25/day) plus mileage. Failure to tender the fee with the subpoena can be grounds to quash. Always include the check with the subpoena.

Pre-Deposition Client Prep — The Staff Checklist

Your job is logistics. The attorney's job is testimony. Never cross the line.

📋 Staff Pre-Deposition Checklist (Client's Depo)

  1. Confirm date/time/location with client — call, confirm in writing, add to client's calendar if possible
  2. Schedule attorney prep session — minimum 2 business days before deposition. Attorney meets with client to review testimony approach.
  3. Dress code guidance — business casual, no political/offensive logos, no sunglasses indoors. This is logistics, not testimony coaching.
  4. Logistics brief — parking, building entry, which floor, arrive 15 minutes early, bring ID
  5. Document review — ensure client has reviewed their own medical records timeline, accident report, and any prior statements they made
  6. Remind client: bring nothing except what attorney says to bring. No phones during deposition.
  7. CasePeer update — log deposition date, prep session date, and any client concerns raised

⚠️ What Staff Can Say vs. What Staff Cannot Say

✅ Staff CAN Say❌ Staff CANNOT Say
"Wear business casual — no jeans or t-shirts""When they ask about the accident, say you were going the speed limit"
"Arrive 15 minutes early and bring your ID""Don't mention the prior accident — it's not relevant"
"The attorney will meet with you Thursday to prepare""If they ask about the treatment gap, just say you couldn't afford it"
"Review your medical records before the prep session""Your strongest point is the rear-end presumption — emphasize that"
"Let the attorney know if you have any concerns""The defense doctor's report is weak — don't worry about their questions on it"
From Essential Lawyering Skills: Client preparation should use "inquiring mode" — help the client express their own truth, not recite a script. The attorney's prep session creates space for the client to practice saying what actually happened, in their own words. Staff's job is to make sure that prep session happens and the client is physically ready.

Post-Deposition: Transcripts, Review & Corrections

The deposition isn't over when the witness leaves the room. The 30-day clock starts now.

📋 Post-Deposition Staff Workflow

StepActionDeadline
1Order the transcript from the court reporter (if not already arranged)Within 24 hours of deposition
2Receive transcript — immediately calendar the 30-day review/signature deadlineUpon receipt
3Forward transcript to attorney for substantive reviewSame day as receipt
4If our client's deposition: schedule client review session. Client reviews transcript and signs or notes corrections on errata sheet.Within 14 days of receipt (leaves buffer)
530-day signature deadline — if the deponent requested to review the transcript (O.C.G.A. § 9-11-30(e)), they have 30 days to sign it. If not signed within 30 days, it may be used as if signed.30 days from transcript delivery
6File signed transcript or errata sheet. Upload to CasePeer document management.Upon completion

📝 Errata Sheets — What They Are and When They Matter

An errata sheet is a formal document listing corrections to a deposition transcript. The deponent identifies the page, line, original text, correction, and reason for each change.

Allowed corrections: Typographical errors, misheard words, technical medical terms transcribed incorrectly.

Risky corrections: Substantive changes to testimony (changing "yes" to "no") — these are technically allowed but will be highlighted by opposing counsel at trial. The attorney must decide whether a substantive correction is worth the impeachment risk.

The 30-day trap: If the 30-day review period expires without a signature, the transcript stands as written. Missing this deadline means the client cannot correct errors — including errors that could hurt the case at trial. Calendar this deadline the INSTANT you receive the transcript.

The 90-Day Trial Prep Timeline

Trial readiness is a 90-day process, not a 48-hour panic. Staff drives the checklist.

⏱ The 90-Day Trial Preparation Timeline

WindowStaff ActionCritical Notes
90 DaysUpdate CasePeer status to Trial Prep. Create trial prep folder. Begin exhibit compilation.Status must change NOW — not at 30 days
75 DaysIdentify ALL witnesses. Begin subpoena preparation for non-party witnesses. Confirm expert availability.Experts book months ahead — check NOW
60 DaysComplete exhibit binder — numbered, indexed, copies for judge, opposing counsel, and witness stand.Three complete sets minimum
45 DaysServe all witness subpoenas. Confirm court reporter for trial. Coordinate AV equipment for exhibits.Subpoenas require service + witness fee
30 DaysAttorney files pre-trial motions (motions in limine, jury charges). Staff assists with exhibit list and witness list filings.Court deadlines vary — check local rules
14 DaysConfirm ALL witnesses. Re-confirm court reporter. Final exhibit check. Client prep session with attorney. Confirm courtroom location and parking.Any unconfirmed witness = RED FLAG
7 DaysFinal logistics check. Pack trial supplies (exhibit binder, sticky notes, timeline poster, client documents). Verify all technology works.The "trial box" should be ready to walk out the door
Day OfArrive 45 minutes early. Set up exhibit station. Confirm client arrival. Have backup copies of everything. Manage witness schedule throughout the day.Staff manages logistics so attorney focuses on advocacy

📁 The Exhibit Binder — Organization Standard

  • Tab 1: Exhibit index — numbered list with brief description of each exhibit
  • Tab 2: Police/accident report
  • Tab 3: Medical records (chronological by provider)
  • Tab 4: Medical bills summary
  • Tab 5: Photographs (accident scene, vehicle damage, injuries)
  • Tab 6: Expert reports
  • Tab 7: Deposition excerpts (key admissions, digested and tabbed)
  • Tab 8: Demonstratives (diagrams, timelines, charts)

Three complete sets: Attorney's working copy, judge's copy, opposing counsel's copy. A fourth "witness stand" copy is best practice.

📊 CasePeer Status Transitions (Litigation)

Discovery → Mediation → Trial Prep → Trial → Post-Trial

Update status at each transition. A file in Discovery status with a trial date 60 days out is a process failure visible on the dashboard.

From Injury Case Playbook: "The ratio of preparation to performance can easily reach 15 or 20 to 1. It might take 10 hours to prepare for a half-hour counseling session with a client." (Essential Lawyering Skills §2.2.6). Trial is the ultimate expression of this principle — 90 days of staff preparation for 3–5 days of trial.
Post-trial procedures: After verdict or mistrial: (1) Collect all exhibits from the courtroom, (2) Preserve the record for appeal — ensure court reporter files the transcript, (3) Update CasePeer to Post-Trial status, (4) Calendar any post-trial motion deadlines (new trial motion = 30 days under O.C.G.A. § 5-5-40).
